# Nintendo Labor Fact-Check: Stable Employment Record

## Executive summary

Nintendo is the only major global game publisher that conducted **zero mass layoffs of permanent employees** in the 2022-2026 period. This is a combination of Japanese labor law protections, consistent profitability driven by the Switch/Switch 2 ecosystem, and a corporate culture that prioritizes long-term employment over cost-cutting.

## Claims assessment

### "Nintendo has never done mass layoffs" — TRUE

There are no documented cases of mass layoffs of permanent (seishain) employees at Nintendo Co., Ltd. (Japan) in its history as a video game company. This includes the 2022-2026 period when Sony (~900 layoffs), Microsoft (6,100+ gaming layoffs), and virtually every major publisher cut thousands of jobs.

### "Nintendo has industry-low turnover" — TRUE

- **Japan:** 1.9% turnover rate; average employee tenure 14.4 years (as of 2025)
- **US (Nintendo of America):** 5.1% turnover; average tenure ~10 years
- **Industry comparison:** US game industry average turnover is significantly higher (estimated 15-25% depending on role)
- Source: Nintendo's own published employee data (as of 2025)

### "Japanese labor law makes layoffs nearly impossible" — TRUE

Japanese labor law imposes the strictest layoff standards in the developed world for regular employees. Under the "four criteria" (四大要件) developed by Japanese courts:

1. **Necessity:** Employer must prove severe business necessity for headcount reduction
2. **Avoidance duty:** Employer must exhaust all alternatives (transfers, voluntary retirement, hiring freeze, reduced hours)
3. **Reasonableness:** Employee selection criteria must be objective and reasonable
4. **Procedural fairness:** Employer must consult with labor representatives or unions

Nintendo has never triggered these criteria because it has been consistently profitable.

### "Nintendo pays lower than US/UK peers" — TRUE

Japanese game developer salaries are structurally lower than US/UK equivalents. Industry estimates:
- **Japan game developer average:** ¥4-6 million/year (~$28K-$42K USD)
- **Nintendo Japan:** Estimated ¥5-8 million/year (~$35K-$56K USD) — above Japanese industry average but well below US
- **US game developer average:** $80K-$120K+
- However, Nintendo Japan offers strong benefits, stable lifetime employment, biannual bonuses (typically 4-6 months salary total), housing allowances, and annual pay increases

### "Strong benefits compensate for lower pay" — TRUE

Nintendo Japan provides:
- Lifetime employment (effectively)
- Annual base wage increases (2-5% annually, including 2022 and 2023 increases)
- Biannual bonuses (summer and winter, typically 2-3 months salary each)
- Housing allowance
- Commuting allowance
- Company housing/dormitories (for some employees)
- Near 100% parental leave return rate
- Employee stock ownership plan (ESOP)

### "No union in Japan is normal" — TRUE

The Japanese game industry has minimal union presence. While Japan has strong enterprise unions (kigyō-betsu kumiai) at many large companies, Nintendo has never had one. Key context:

- **Neither Nintendo nor any of its Japanese subsidiaries** (Monolith Soft, Nintendo EPD) have a union
- **Japanese enterprise unions** operate differently from Western trade unions — they are company-specific, cooperate with management, and rarely strike
- **The broader Japanese game industry** has almost no union presence (unlike Korea, where major companies like Nexon have unions)
- **Key exception:** Creators' Guild of Japan exists but has minimal presence in game development

### "Nintendo's Japan labor model contrasts with NOA" — TRUE

Nintendo of America has been the site of:
- NLRB complaint (2022, settled for $25,910)
- Toxic culture allegations (Kotaku 2022 investigation)
- Contractor layoffs (~100+ in 2024)
- New NLRB complaints filed (2025/2026) via Teksystems

This creates a split labor profile where Japanese permanent staff have near-guaranteed employment while US contractors face precarity. The NOA full-time staff turnover (5.1%) is still low by US industry standards, but the contractor system creates a two-tier workforce.

## Key takeaway for union organizing

Nintendo Japan is unlikely to organize in the foreseeable future due to: (1) effective lifetime employment removing the primary motivation for unionization, (2) Japanese enterprise union culture that doesn't map to Western organizing models, (3) strong management trust (evidenced by 1.9% turnover). However, Nintendo of America represents a continuing organizing opportunity, with fresh NLRB complaints in 2025/2026 signaling ongoing worker discontent despite the settled 2022 case.
