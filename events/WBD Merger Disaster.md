# Event: WBD Merger Disaster ($5B+ Debt)

## Event Summary

The Warner Bros. Discovery merger, completed in April 2022, created one of the most heavily indebted media companies in history. The combined entity carried more than **$50 billion in debt**, stemming primarily from AT&T's disastrous $85B acquisition of Time Warner in 2018 (later spun off as WarnerMedia).

## Timeline

| Date | Event |
|------|-------|
| 2018 | AT&T acquires Time Warner for $85B (including $40B in debt) |
| May 2021 | AT&T announces spin-off merger of WarnerMedia with Discovery |
| April 8, 2022 | Merger completed, Warner Bros. Discovery formed |
| 2022 | WBD begins cost-cutting: $1B downsizing plan announced |
| 2022–2024 | Multiple rounds of layoffs across all divisions (total ~15,000+ jobs cut) |
| Aug 2022 | Batgirl film cancelled for tax write-down ($70M+) |
| 2022–2023 | Content write-offs: hundreds of millions in cancelled/reduced projects |
| Dec 12, 2024 | WBD restructures into two divisions: Streaming & Studios and Global Linear Networks |
| June 2025 | Paramount Skydance announces acquisition of WBD |
| 2026 | Paramount Skydance acquisition finalized, WBD ceases to exist as independent entity |

## Key Details

- **Debt load:** $50B+ at merger; reduced to ~$37B by mid-2025 but still massive
- **David Zaslav's strategy:** Aggressive cost-cutting, debt reduction via free cash flow, content write-offs, layoffs. Zaslav tied his compensation to free cash flow (not stock price) in 2023.
- **Impact on Games Division:** WB Games was pressured to generate reliable revenue but also required to cut costs. This led to the cancellation of smaller projects, closure of studios (Monolith, Player First, WB San Diego), and a refocus on a few major IPs.
- **Layoffs:** WBD cut between 15,000–20,000 jobs across all divisions from 2022–2025. Games division saw repeated layoff rounds.
- **Notable:** The Batgirl cancellation ($70M+ write-off) and Scoob! Holiday Haunt cancellations became symbols of WBD's slash-and-burn approach.

## Impact on Game Studios

- Directly caused the restructuring of WB Games to focus on 4 core IPs
- Suicide Squad's $200M flop was especially damaging given the debt-sensitive environment
- Contributed to the closure of Monolith Productions, Player First Games, and WB Games San Diego in Feb 2025
- Left remaining studios under constant threat of further cuts
