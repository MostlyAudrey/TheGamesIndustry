# Japan Studio reorganization (2021)

Sony Interactive Entertainment restructured its historic Japan Studio in Tokyo, effectively ending internal AAA game development in its home market. The process unfolded over several months in early 2021.

**Timeline:**

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| Late 2020 | Sony briefs staff on restructuring plans |
| Feb 25, 2021 | Bloomberg reports key staff departures: Gravity Rush creator Keiichiro Toyama, art director Masami Yamamoto, and producer Junya Okura leave to form Bokeh Game Studio |
| Feb 26, 2021 | GamesIndustry.biz and VGC confirm Japan Studio restructuring |
| Apr 1, 2021 | Team Asobi becomes standalone SIE subsidiary; Japan Studio effectively ends internal development |
| Dec 2021 | Sony confirms Japan Studio will focus on IP management and external production support, not game development |

**Causes:**
- Sony's strategic pivot to Western AAA market following the success of The Last of Us, God of War, Spider-Man
- Japan Studio's limited commercial output relative to Western first-party studios
- Key talent departures and difficulty retaining creative leads
- Shuhei Yoshida's 2019 departure from Worldwide Studios

**Impact:**
- Japan Studio's role as Sony's co-development partner with Japanese studios (including FromSoftware on Bloodborne/Demon's Souls) effectively ended
- Team Asobi (Astro Bot series) emerged as Sony's sole remaining Japanese first-party studio
- The reorganization symbolized Sony's retreat from the Japanese console development market
- Team Asobi's success with Astro Bot (2024) later demonstrated the value of Sony's remaining Japanese development capacity

**Labor:**
- No involuntary mass layoffs of permanent employees
- ~300 net reduction from ~400 peak headcount to ~100-150 Team Asobi
- Key creative departures: Toyama, Okura, Yamamoto → founded Bokeh Game Studio (which became part of NetEase in 2022)
- Some contract staff not renewed
