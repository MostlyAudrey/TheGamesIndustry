# CyberConnect2 goes independent (July 2021)

CEO Hiroshi Matsuyama spent approximately ¥20 billion (~$180M) of CyberConnect2's cash reserves to buy back majority shares held by external investors, returning the studio to full independence.

**Background:** CyberConnect2 had accepted external investment during its rapid growth phase (2014-2019), during which it opened multiple studios (Tokyo, Osaka, Montreal, Seoul) and expanded its workforce to ~300 employees. The studio was primarily doing work-for-hire licensed games (Naruto, Dragon Ball, Demon Slayer).

**Motivation:** Matsuyama stated he wanted to control the company's direction and prioritize original IP development over publisher-commissioned work. The buyback allowed CyberConnect2 to:
- Refuse projects that didn't align with creative goals
- Self-fund original IP development (Fuga: Melodies of Steel trilogy)
- Operate without investor pressure for short-term returns

**Outcome:** Following the buyback, CyberConnect2 focused on original IP (Fuga series while continuing select anime licensed projects. The studio has remained independent through 2026.

**Significance:** The ¥20B self-buyout is an unusually aggressive independence move in the Japanese game industry, where most mid-tier studios have accepted acquisition or external investment (e.g., FromSoftware → Kadokawa/Sony/Tencent; Grasshopper → NetEase; Tango → Krafton).
