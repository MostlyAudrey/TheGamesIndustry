# Marc Petit — GM, Unreal Engine at Epic Games

**The executive leading Unreal Engine's expansion beyond gaming.** Petit runs the Unreal Engine business at Epic Games, overseeing the engine's product strategy, licensing model, and enterprise partnerships across gaming, film, television, automotive, and architecture.

## Career arc

- **Pre-Epic (1990s–2010s):** Held leadership roles across the computer graphics and media industries. Details include senior positions at Autodesk, where he was involved in 3D design and visualization software.
- **2019:** Joined Epic Games as General Manager of the Unreal Engine product line.
- **2022 (April):** Led the commercial launch of Unreal Engine 5 — featuring Nanite (virtualized micropolygon geometry) and Lumen (dynamic global illumination). UE5 was widely adopted across AAA game development.
- **2020–2023:** Expanded Unreal Engine's use in film and television production (The Mandalorian's StageCraft virtual production used UE), automotive visualization, and architectural design.
- **2024–2026:** Continues overseeing Unreal Engine's growth, including the Unreal Editor for Fortnite (UEFN), the Unreal Engine Marketplace, and MetaHuman technology.
- Petit is a frequent speaker at GDC, Unreal Fest, and Siggraph, discussing the engine's roadmap, licensing terms, and cross-industry applications.

## Key achievements

- **UE5 launch:** One of the most significant software releases in game industry history, with Nanite and Lumen redefining real-time graphics capabilities.
- **Cross-industry expansion:** Under Petit, Unreal Engine became the default real-time 3D tool across industries beyond gaming — a multi-billion dollar addressable market.
- **Licensing model shifts:** Managed Unreal Engine's transition to a royalty-based model (5% of gross revenue over $1M) and later, the introduction of the Epic Games Store revenue share incentives.

## Labour context

- Petit's tenure at Epic has coincided with major company changes: the Apple/Epic antitrust lawsuit, Fortnite's continued dominance, and the company's 2023 restructuring which cut 830 jobs (16% of workforce).
- The Unreal Engine team was impacted by the 2023 layoffs, though the exact numbers were not disclosed by division.
- No union presence at Epic Games.
- Epic has an unusual corporate structure, with CEO [[people/Tim Sweeney]] owning ~52% of the company and Tencent owning a 40% stake.
