# Bobby Kotick — CEO, Activision Blizzard (1991-2023)

**CEO of Activision / Activision Blizzard for 32 years — from near-bankruptcy to the largest game acquisition in history.** Departed December 29, 2023 after the Microsoft $68.7B acquisition closed.

## Career arc

- **1991:** At age 28, Kotick and a partner took over struggling Activision. The company was near bankruptcy; Kotick turned it around by restructuring and refocusing.
- **1990s-2000s:** Built Activision through aggressive acquisition — bought 11 game developers in 18 months at one point. Titles included Tony Hawk's Pro Skater, Call of Duty (via Infinity Ward acquisition).
- **2003:** Took Activision public again on NASDAQ.
- **2007:** Engineered the $18.9B merger with Vivendi Games (Blizzard) — creating Activision Blizzard. The merger brought World of Warcraft, Diablo, and StarCraft under his control.
- **2013:** Led the $8.2B buyback from Vivendi, taking Activision Blizzard independent again.
- **2016:** Acquired King Digital for $5.9B — adding Candy Crush and mobile expertise.
- **2021-2023:** Crisis period — DFEH lawsuit, employee walkouts, union organizing, Microsoft acquisition announced.
- **2023-12:** Departed with an estimated $400M+ exit package.

## DFEH lawsuit and toxic culture

The California Department of Fair Employment and Housing filed suit against Activision Blizzard on July 20, 2021, alleging a systemic "frat boy" culture of sexual harassment, discrimination against women, and unequal pay. The lawsuit triggered a crisis:

- **July 28, 2021:** ~2,600+ employees walked out — organized by A Better ABK.
- **Company response:** Initially dismissed the lawsuit as "distorted and false"; later walked back.
- **Brack departed:** Blizzard president J. Allen Brack left in August 2021.
- **WSJ reporting (Nov 2021):** Detailed Kotick's knowledge of misconduct and alleged threats against a female assistant; employees and shareholders called for his resignation.
- **Kotick's response:** Said the WSJ portrayal was "inaccurate and misleading" but acknowledged the company "didn't do a good job of addressing" workplace issues.
- **Reforms:** New HR policies, B | E Better initiative, increased staffing for investigation teams, zero-tolerance harassment policy.
- **Settlements:** $18M with SEC (2022); $54M with California (2023).
- **Long-term impact:** The DFEH lawsuit catalyzed the union movement that organized 4,000+ ABK workers under Microsoft's post-acquisition neutrality agreement.

## Union stance

Kotick's public posture toward organized labor was explicitly oppositional:

- In a **November 2022** investor call, Kotick called the Raven Software QA union campaign "disturbing" and stated that he "never really gave much thought" to employees organizing. Though he said "it's not illegal" and "we don't want to break the law," the characterization of unionization as disturbing was widely cited.
- Kotick-era Activision Blizzard had **no union relationships** — zero collective bargaining agreements across all 32 years of his tenure.
- The company's anti-union posture was cited by CWA as a reason the Microsoft neutrality agreement and acquisition were necessary.
- Workers forming A Better ABK explicitly called for Kotick's resignation — a demand he never met.

## Post-departure

After Kotick's departure, Microsoft Gaming CEO Phil Spencer assumed oversight. The union wave accelerated dramatically under the labor neutrality agreement — the landscape Kotick left behind transformed into the most unionized workforce in US game industry history.
