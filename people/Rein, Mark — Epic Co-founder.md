# Mark Rein — Co-founder, Epic Games

**The business face of Epic Games and Unreal Engine.** Mark Rein co-founded Epic Games alongside [[people/Tim Sweeney]] and has been the company's highest-profile business development and industry relations executive for decades.

## Career arc

- **1990s:** Co-founded Epic Games (originally Potomac Computer Systems) with Tim Sweeney.
- **1998 (Unreal):** Following the release of Unreal, Rein became a leading evangelist for licensing the Unreal Engine to other developers — a strategy that made Epic the preeminent engine provider in the industry.
- **2000–2010:** Established Unreal Engine as the industry standard middleware for AAA development. Licensed to hundreds of studios including major franchises (Gears of War, BioShock, Mass Effect, Batman: Arkham).
- **2010–present:** With the launch of UE4 (2014) and UE5 (2022), Epic's licensing model shifted to subscription/royalty — Rein was central to communicating this transition to developers.
- Rein has been a regular presence at GDC, Unreal Fest, Siggraph, and other industry events, providing monthly updates in Game Developer magazine about Unreal Engine's progress.
- He is also a minority co-owner of the NHL's Carolina Hurricanes.
- He is often the most accessible Epic executive for developer relations and press comments about Unreal Engine's business model.

## Key achievements

- **Unreal Engine licensing pioneer:** Convincing developers to build on a third-party engine was a radical idea in the 1990s, when most studios built proprietary engines. Rein's evangelism created the modern middleware market.
- **Industry diplomacy:** One of the most visible figures at game industry events, Rein has represented Epic in countless developer relations and partnership contexts for over 25 years.

## Labour context

- Rein is a business-side executive who has not been involved in labor issues.
- Epic Games has seen significant layoffs (830 in September 2023), which impacted the Unreal Engine team as well as Fortnite and other divisions.
- No union presence at Epic.
- The company has been publicly critical of unionization in tech, though its formal stance shifted toward neutrality in the wake of 2023 layoffs.
