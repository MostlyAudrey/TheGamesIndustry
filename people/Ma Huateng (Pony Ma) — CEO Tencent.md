# Ma Huateng (Pony Ma)

Ma Huateng, also known as Pony Ma (born October 29, 1971, in Hainan Province, China), is the co-founder, chairman, and CEO of [[orgs/Tencent]], one of the world's largest technology and gaming conglomerates. He earned a B.S. in Computer Science from Shenzhen University in 1993.

## Career Arc

- Co-founded Tencent in 1998 with Zhang Zhidong (Tony Zhang), Chen Yidan, Xu Chenye, and Zeng Liqing
- Launched Tencent's first product OICQ (later QQ), an instant messaging platform that became dominant in China
- Led Tencent's expansion into online games, social media (WeChat/Weixin), fintech, cloud, and entertainment
- Under his leadership, Tencent became the largest gaming company in the world by revenue, with major stakes in [[orgs/Riot Games]], [[orgs/Epic Games]], [[orgs/Activision Blizzard]], [[orgs/Ubisoft]], [[orgs/Supercell]], and hundreds of other game studios globally
- As of 2026, Ma is one of the wealthiest people in China and Asia

## Key Decisions in Gaming

- Pushed Tencent's "publishing + investment" dual strategy in games, leading to ownership or major stakes in Riot Games (100%), Epic Games (40%), Supercell (84%), and minority stakes in Activision Blizzard, Ubisoft, and FromSoftware
- Championed QQ and WeChat as distribution platforms for casual and mobile games
- Survived and recovered from China's 2018-2019 game license freeze and 2021 gaming crackdown
- Navigated increasing Chinese government regulation of gaming (play time limits for minors, content restrictions)

## Labor Context

- Tencent is known for competitive compensation but also a high-pressure "996" culture (9am-9pm, 6 days/week), though enforcement varies
- Tencent has faced criticism over working conditions at portfolio companies, particularly Riot Games (2018-2019 walkouts, discrimination lawsuits)
- No formal union presence at Tencent — Chinese labor laws restrict independent unions
- Tencent has invested in employee well-being programs and anti-crunch initiatives in some studios
