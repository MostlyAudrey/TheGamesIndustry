# Jonathan Blow — Braid, The Witness, Thekla Inc

**The indie auteur who set the template for self-funded, high-ambition independent game development.** Blow's Braid (2008) was one of the first indie games to achieve both critical acclaim and commercial success on digital platforms, helping establish the viability of independently published games.

## Career arc

- **1990s:** Studied computer science and English at UC Berkeley (dropped out). Founded a game startup during dot-com boom.
- **2000s:** Worked as game-development contractor after startup closure. Co-founded Experimental Gameplay Workshop. Wrote monthly column for Game Developer magazine.
- **2004–2008:** Developed Braid — a 2D platformer with time-manipulation mechanics. Self-funded through consulting work.
- **2008 (August):** Released Braid on Xbox Live Arcade (later PC). $15 price point was considered high for XBLA but it sold 55,000 copies in its first week ($800k revenue). Sold 2M+ copies lifetime.
- **2009:** Featured prominently in Indie Game: The Movie alongside Phil Fish and Edmund McMillen.
- **2009–2016:** Founded Thekla Inc. Developed The Witness — a first-person puzzle game on a remote island. Development funded entirely by Braid profits and took ~7 years.
- **2016:** Released The Witness — critically acclaimed, commercially successful (though less than Braid relative to its budget).
- **2017–present:** Became frustrated with C++; began designing Jai, a new programming language optimized for game development. Jai compiler in beta as of 2026.
- Blow has also been critical of aspects of modern game design and has caused public controversy with political statements.

## Key games

| Game | Year | Significance |
|---|---|---|
| Braid | 2008 | Landmark indie; defined the "indie game" as a commercial category |
| The Witness | 2016 | Massive puzzle world; critical acclaim |
| Jai (language) | In beta | Game programming language designed by Blow |

- **Self-funding model:** Blow proved an indie developer could fund their own expensive, long-term project with profits from an earlier hit — a model later followed by others.
- **Custom engines:** Both Braid (DotNet) and The Witness (custom C++ engine) used proprietary technology — Blow advocates for understanding your tools deeply.
- **Anti-crunch views:** Blow has publicly criticized the game industry's crunch culture and exploitative labor practices.
- **Controversy:** In the 2020s, Blow expressed controversial political views on social media, particularly around COVID-19 and scientific institutions, leading to public criticism.

## Labour context

- Thekla operates as a tiny team (single digits). Blow funds all development personally.
- Blow has argued that the traditional AAA studio model is structurally broken — he prefers small teams, no publisher deadlines, no external investors dictating creative decisions.
- He has been critical of the "games-as-service" model and live-service monetization strategies that require continuous labour from development teams.
- No union presence at Thekla. The team is too small for formal union organizing, though Blow has expressed sympathy for anti-corporate labour critiques.
