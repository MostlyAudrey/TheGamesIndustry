# Shigeru Miyamoto — Representative Director / Creative Fellow, Nintendo

**The most famous game designer alive.** Creator of Mario, Donkey Kong, The Legend of Zelda, Star Fox, Pikmin, and other defining franchises of the medium. At Nintendo since 1977 — a 49-year career at one company, emblematic of Japanese lifetime employment.

## Career arc

- **1977:** Hired by Nintendo as a staff artist. Early work on arcade cabinet art.
- **1981:** Created Donkey Kong — Nintendo's first major arcade hit, introducing Mario (then 'Jumpman').
- **1985:** Super Mario Bros. for NES — redefined the platforming genre and saved the North American video game industry after the 1983 crash.
- **1986:** The Legend of Zelda — introduced open-world exploration, battery saves, and non-linear gameplay.
- **1990s:** Produced a string of landmark titles: Super Mario World, The Legend of Zelda: A Link to the Past, Star Fox, Super Mario 64, The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time.
- **2002:** Promoted to Senior Managing Director of Nintendo; head of Nintendo EAD (Entertainment Analysis & Development) — oversaw all first-party software development.
- **2012:** Shifted from day-to-day production to higher-level creative oversight.
- **2015:** Officially stepped back from game development to oversee broader Nintendo entertainment initiatives.
- **2017:** Named "Creative Fellow" — a role described as semi-retired but still deeply involved.
- **2023:** Consulted on The Super Mario Bros. Movie — Illumination/Nintendo co-production that grossed $1.36B worldwide.

## Japanese labor context

Miyamoto's career exists entirely within the Japanese labor system. Key contrasts with US/European game industry:

- **Japanese lifetime employment:** Layoffs of permanent full-time employees are effectively illegal under Japanese law absent severe financial distress. Nintendo has never conducted mass layoffs of permanent staff.
- **Nintendo retention:** 1.9% annual turnover in Japan; 98.1% retention rate; average employee tenure 14.4 years (as of 2025, [[sources/2025-07-22 — thegamer-nintendo-staff-retention]]).
- **No union organizing:** No documented union campaigns, certification elections, or collective bargaining at Nintendo Co., Ltd.
- **Contractor dependency:** Like other Japanese publishers, Nintendo uses contractors (see NOA's "red badge" system) for non-permanent roles — labor flexibility at the margins while core workforce is protected.
- **Salary transparency:** Nintendo increased base wages in 2022 and 2024 in response to inflation and competitive pressure; 100% of Japan employees report regular career growth conversations.

Miyamoto has never publicly commented on unionization, labor organizing, or workplace rights.

## Role in the Nintendo power structure

Miyamoto holds the title of **Representative Director** — a formal board position at Nintendo Co., Ltd. He is the company's most senior creative voice but does not set corporate strategy or labor policy. CEO Shuntaro Furukawa (since 2018) is the ultimate decision-maker, alongside co-founders and legacy leadership.
