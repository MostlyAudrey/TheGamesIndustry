# David Jaffe — Creator of God of War and Twisted Metal

**The designer who created two of PlayStation's most iconic franchises.** David Jaffe directed the original God of War (2005) and created the Twisted Metal series — both defining PlayStation brands.

## Career arc

- **1990s:** Began career in game development. Worked at Sony Interactive Studios America.
- **1995:** Created Twisted Metal for the original PlayStation — a vehicular combat game that became a hit franchise.
- **1995–2000:** Worked on multiple Twisted Metal sequels.
- **2002–2005:** Led development of God of War at Santa Monica Studio. Inspired by the Prince of Persia: The Sands of Time formula, Jaffe's God of War introduced Kratos and the "hack-and-slash" cinematic action genre.
- **2005 (March):** God of War released to critical and commercial success.
- **2007:** Left Sony to co-found Eat Sleep Play (with Scott Campbell).
- **2008–2011:** Eat Sleep Play developed Twisted Metal (PS3 reboot, 2011) for Sony.
- **2012:** Left Eat Sleep Play.
- **2012–present:** Freelance game development, consulting, and streaming. Has been developing smaller independent projects.
- Jaffe has become a prominent (and controversial) figure on streaming platforms, giving unfiltered commentary on game development, industry politics, and culture.

- **God of War's genesis:** Jaffe pitched God of War as "Prince of Persia meets Greek mythology with a massive sense of scale." His direction set the tone for the entire franchise.
- **Twisted Metal's cult status:** The vehicular combat series was one of PlayStation's first iconic exclusive franchises.
- **Unfiltered commentary:** Jaffe has become a controversial, outspoken commentator on industry issues, often taking contrarian positions on labour, diversity, and game design.

## Labour context

- Jaffe left Sony in 2007 to pursue independent development, citing the desire for creative freedom outside the corporate structure.
- At Santa Monica Studio, Jaffe led a team that grew significantly during God of War development — the standard AAA studio model of the mid-2000s.
- Eat Sleep Play (his co-founded studio) gave him independence but had mixed commercial results.
- In recent streaming commentary, Jaffe has discussed the realities of AAA development, including publisher pressure and team management challenges.
- No union presence at any of his studios. Jaffe has made controversial statements about unionization (generally skeptical).
- His departure from AAA to small-scale independent work illustrates the tension between creative ambition and corporate constraints.
