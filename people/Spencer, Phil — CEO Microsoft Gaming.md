# Phil Spencer — CEO, Microsoft Gaming

**CEO of Microsoft Gaming since 2022.** Oversaw the transition of Xbox from a console business to a multiplatform publisher and subscription service (Game Pass). The most consequential executive for organized labor in the game industry — under his leadership, Microsoft signed the neutrality agreement that enabled 4,000+ union members.

## Career arc

- **1988:** Joined Microsoft as an intern. No birth year publicly known.
- **Early career:** Rose through Microsoft's internal ranks across CD-ROMs, Microsoft Home, and gaming divisions.
- **2008:** Took leadership role in Microsoft Game Studios / Xbox division.
- **2014:** Named head of Xbox after Don Mattrick departed for Zynga.
- **2017:** Launched Xbox Game Pass — subscription service that reshaped Microsoft's gaming strategy.
- **2021:** Oversaw $7.5B ZeniMax/Bethesda acquisition.
- **2022:** Promoted to CEO of Microsoft Gaming; oversaw announcement and closing of $68.7B Activision Blizzard acquisition.
- **2024-present:** Oversaw two major layoff rounds (1,900 in Jan 2024; 3,200 announced Jul 2026).

## Key relationships

- Reports to Satya Nadella (Microsoft CEO)
- Oversaw Bobby Kotick's post-acquisition departure (Dec 2023)
- Worked with CWA President Chris Shelton on neutrality agreement
- Studio leadership under him includes Matt Booty (Xbox Game Studios president), Sarah Bond (Xbox president)

## Labor legacy

Spencer's labor legacy is sharply bifurcated:

**Pre-expiration (2022-Fall 2025):** The 2022 CWA neutrality agreement enabled the single largest unionization wave in US game industry history. Raven Software QA organized and won a first contract. Activision QA United formed with 600 members. Blizzard Entertainment organized wall-to-wall across multiple business units — WoW, Overwatch, Diablo, Story & Franchise, Platform & Technology — reaching ~1,900 members who won a landmark first contract in September 2026 with AI language, remote work guarantee, and recall rights.

**Post-expiration (Fall 2025-present):** The neutrality agreement's expiration and the July 2026 layoff of 3,200 gaming workers (including union members) fundamentally changed the relationship. Activision QA United — whose workforce declined from 600 to 400 — authorized a strike in October 2026 after 2.5 years without a first contract. CWA has publicly criticized Microsoft's post-neutrality posture.
