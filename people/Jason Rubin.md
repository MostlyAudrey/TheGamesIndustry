# Jason Rubin

**Role:** Former VP of Content, Oculus/Meta; Co-founder, Naughty Dog  
**Orgs:** [[Oculus VR]], [[Meta]], [[Naughty Dog]], [[Facebook]]

## Career Arc

Jason Rubin is a veteran game developer best known as co-founder of **Naughty Dog**, where he and Andy Gavin created iconic franchises including *Crash Bandicoot*, *Jak and Daxter*, and the early *Uncharted* series. After two decades at Naughty Dog, Rubin left in the early 2010s.

In 2014, Rubin joined **Oculus** as Head of Worldwide Studios, becoming a key figure in VR content investment and strategy. He guided Oculus's content acquisitions, first-party development, and third-party publishing. After Facebook's rebrand to Meta, Rubin became VP of Metaverse Content. He left Meta in March 2026 after 12 years, having held titles including VP of Content, VP of Special Gaming Strategies, and VP of Metaverse Content.

## Key Decisions

- **Co-founded Naughty Dog (1984):** Created one of gaming's most celebrated studios.
- **Joined Oculus early (2014):** Brought AAA game development expertise to VR content strategy.
- **Led VR content investment:** Oversaw funding and publishing of dozens of VR titles, making Oculus a content powerhouse.
- **Stayed through Meta era:** Navigated the transition from Oculus to Facebook to Meta.
- **Departed Meta (2026):** Left after 12 years at Oculus/Facebook/Meta.

## Labor Context

Rubin's career spans multiple eras of game development — from indie startup (Naughty Dog as two guys), to AAA console development (Sony partnership), to corporate VR (Facebook/Meta). He has been an advocate for developer-friendly policies in VR content deals and spoke about the importance of creator autonomy within large platforms.
