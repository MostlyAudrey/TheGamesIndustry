# Gabe Newell — Co-founder and Owner, Valve Corporation

**Co-founder of Valve (1996), creator of Steam (2003), and majority owner of the most valuable private company in games.** Born 1962. Newell controls the dominant PC gaming platform (Steam), developer of Half-Life, Dota 2, Counter-Strike 2, and the Steam Deck handheld.

## Career arc

- **1983-1996:** Microsoft employee — spent 13 years at Microsoft, rising to become a "Microsoft Millionaire" from pre-IPO stock. Left to start Valve.
- **1996:** Co-founded Valve Corporation with Mike Harrington in Kirkland, Washington.
- **1998:** Released Half-Life — one of the most critically acclaimed FPS games, established Valve's reputation.
- **2000:** Harrington sold his stake to Newell; Newell became sole owner (controlling >50% equity).
- **2003:** Launched Steam — initially a patch-delivery system for Counter-Strike, evolved into the dominant PC game storefront.
- **2004:** Half-Life 2 — bundled with Steam (controversially requiring online activation), but cemented Steam's user base.
- **2012:** Valve officially eliminated formal management structure — adopted flat hierarchy with no managers, no job titles.
- **2015:** Announced Half-Life: Alyx for VR (released 2020) — Valve's return to flagship development.
- **2022-present:** Steam Deck launch; legal challenges including antitrust class action; NY AG loot box lawsuit.

## Valve structure and ownership

Valve is a **private company** with an unusual ownership structure:
- **Gabe Newell** holds majority control (>50%)
- Remaining equity held by **early employees and executives** via private grants
- **No outside investors** — never raised venture capital or outside equity
- **No IPO** — Newell has repeatedly stated Valve will remain private
- **No board of directors** — Newell is the sole decision-maker
- **Estimated value:** Steam's estimated $16.2B revenue (2025) with ~350 employees implies extraordinary per-employee value

## Labor profile

Valve is a unique case in the games industry labor landscape:

- **No union:** No organizing efforts, no certification elections, no union presence at any level.
- **No mass layoffs:** No documented layoff events in the 2022-2026 period — extremely rare in the industry.
- **Flat hierarchy:** Since 2012, Valve has operated with no formal managers, no job titles, and a self-directed project allocation system. Employees choose their own projects.
- **High compensation:** Per leaked court documents, ~$1.3M mean gross pay per employee (2021). Total payroll $450M for ~350 employees. Among the highest per-employee pay in tech.
- **Critiques:** Former employees report the flat hierarchy can feel "like high school" (Kotaku). The peer-review compensation system is described as "nebulous and constantly changing" (Glassdoor). Informal power structures emerge in the absence of formal hierarchy.
- **Headcount:** ~350-384 employees — remarkably small for a company controlling the dominant PC storefront. Revelio Labs reports ~384 as of 2026.
- **Location:** Bellevue, Washington — Washington State has weaker labor protections than California.

## Legal exposure

Valve faces significant legal risk that could reshape its business model:

1. **In re Valve Antitrust Litigation** — Class action certified Nov 2024, covering ~32,000 game developers. Allegations: Steam's 30% commission and most-favored-nation pricing constitute illegal monopolization. Ongoing.
2. **New York v. Valve Corp.** — NY AG Letitia James lawsuit (Feb 2026) alleging loot boxes constitute illegal gambling. Ongoing.
3. **UK class action** — £656M claim alleging Valve's pricing restrictions violate UK competition law. Ongoing.

## Public persona

- Known for being famously private about Valve's finances and operations.
- Spends significant time in New Zealand; relocated there during COVID-19 pandemic.
- Public spokesperson for Steam and Valve's positions on PC gaming, Linux gaming, and antitrust.
- No significant public statements on labor organizing or unionization.

## Quotes

- "We wouldn't want to be in business five years from now if we had to put up with our internal dysfunction." — Newell on Valve's flat hierarchy (various interviews)
