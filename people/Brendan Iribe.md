# Brendan Iribe

**Role:** Co-founder & former CEO, Oculus VR  
**Orgs:** [[Oculus VR]], [[Meta]] (formerly Facebook), [[Scaleform]]

## Career Arc

Brendan Trexler Iribe (born August 12, 1979, Maryland) is an American game programmer, entrepreneur, and co-founder of **Oculus VR**. He briefly attended the University of Maryland, College Park before dropping out to do freelance programming work. He co-founded **Scaleform** (a UI middleware company sold to Autodesk in 2011) before co-founding Oculus VR with Palmer Luckey, Nate Mitchell, and Michael Antonov in 2012.

Iribe served as Oculus's original CEO and led the company through its $2B+ acquisition by Facebook in 2014. He stayed at Facebook through the integration period, eventually stepping down as CEO in 2016, moving to a PC-focused VR role, and departing fully in late 2020. He has since focused on philanthropy, including a major donation to the University of Maryland's Brendan Iribe Center for Computer Science and Engineering.

## Key Decisions

- **Co-founded Oculus VR (2012):** Helped bring the Oculus Rift from prototype to consumer product.
- **Led Facebook acquisition (2014):** Navigated the landmark $2B+ deal, one of the largest gaming/tech acquisitions.
- **Stepped down as CEO (2016):** Transitioned leadership as Oculus integrated deeper into Facebook.
- **Major tech philanthropy:** Funded the Iribe Center at University of Maryland, supporting CS education.

## Labor Context

The Facebook/Oculus acquisition raised significant labor and cultural questions. Many Oculus employees navigated the transition from startup equity wealth to corporate environment. Iribe's departure, like other co-founders, reflected the difficulty of preserving startup culture inside a large platform company.
