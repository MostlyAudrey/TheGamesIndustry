# Riccardo Zacconi

**Role:** Co-founder & former CEO, King  
**Orgs:** [[King]], [[Activision Blizzard]], [[Microsoft]] (post-acquisition), [[Sweet Capital]] (venture fund)

## Career Arc

Riccardo Zacconi is an Italian-born businessman who co-founded **King** in 2003 and served as its CEO for over a decade. Born May 11, 1967 in Rome, Zacconi earned a degree in economics from LUISS University in 1991. He began his career in management consulting before moving to the UK in 2001 to pursue digital investments.

Under Zacconi's leadership, King launched *Candy Crush Saga* in 2012, which became one of the most successful mobile games of all time, generating billions in revenue and reaching over 200M active users. King went public in 2014 and was acquired by Activision Blizzard in 2016 for $5.9B. Zacconi stepped down as CEO in 2015. He later founded **Sweet Capital**, a venture fund investing in gaming and entertainment.

## Key Decisions

- **Focus on casual, accessible gameplay:** King's design philosophy centered on "bite-sized" gaming sessions that anyone could pick up.
- **Cross-platform launch strategy:** *Candy Crush Saga* launched on Facebook before mobile, building a massive social audience that transferred seamlessly to iOS/Android.
- **IPO timing (2014):** Took King public at a peak valuation before the mobile gaming market matured, netting significant returns.
- **Sale to Activision Blizzard (2016):** Sold King for $5.9B, integrating into the larger Activision ecosystem.
- **Freemium model mastery:** Pioneered the monetization of mobile puzzle/casual games through in-app purchases and "lives" mechanics.

## Labor Context

King has faced labor scrutiny typical of the mobile gaming industry, including concerns around crunch during major updates and the psychological impact of designing monetization loops. Zacconi's leadership era saw King grow from a small startup to a public company with thousands of employees across multiple studios. The company's Swedish and UK studios were considered relatively worker-friendly compared to some peers.
