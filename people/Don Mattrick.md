# Don Mattrick

**Role:** Former CEO, Zynga (2013–2015); Former President, Xbox  
**Orgs:** [[Zynga]], [[Microsoft]] (Xbox), [[Electronic Arts]]

## Career Arc

Don Mattrick is a Canadian-born video game executive with a 30+ year career. At 17, he designed a game with a friend and sold it for $1M. He spent 15 years at **EA Canada** as President of Worldwide Studios, overseeing titles like *Need for Speed* and *FIFA*. In 2007, he joined Microsoft as President of the Interactive Entertainment Business, leading the Xbox 360 and Kinect development.

In July 2013, Mattrick was appointed CEO of **Zynga**, replacing founder Mark Pincus. His tenure was marked by Zynga's pivot from Facebook-focused social games to mobile. He left Zynga in 2015. He has since invested in and advised gaming startups.

## Key Decisions

- **Led Xbox Live and Kinect:** Drove Microsoft's online gaming strategy and motion controls.
- **Left Microsoft for Zynga (2013):** Surprised the industry by leaving Xbox to lead struggling Zynga.
- **Pushed Zynga's mobile transition:** Shifted resources from Facebook to mobile-first development.
- **Restructured Zynga:** Laid off 18% of workforce and closed offices in a turnaround effort.
- **Left Zynga (2015):** Departed after two years as the turnaround showed mixed results.

## Labor Context

Mattrick's tenure at Xbox included the controversial Xbox One launch (DRM, always-online policies) which caused significant backlash and led to Microsoft reversing course. At Zynga, his restructuring and layoffs were painful but seen as necessary for survival. His career illustrates the tension between AAA console and mobile gaming cultures.
