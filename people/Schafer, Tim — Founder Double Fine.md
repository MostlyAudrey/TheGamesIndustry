# Tim Schafer — Founder, Double Fine Productions

**The industry's master of comedy and heart in game writing.** Schafer spent a decade at LucasArts creating or co-creating iconic adventure games (Day of the Tentacle, Full Throttle, Grim Fandango) before founding Double Fine, where he made Psychonauts and Psychonauts 2.

## Career arc

- **1989:** Hired at Lucasfilm Games (later LucasArts) after sending a demo of his text adventure game.
- **1990–1992:** Assistant designer on The Secret of Monkey Island and Monkey Island 2: LeChuck's Revenge.
- **1993:** Co-designer on Day of the Tentacle — widely considered one of the greatest adventure games ever made.
- **1995:** Lead designer of Full Throttle — cinematic motorcycle gang adventure.
- **1998:** Lead designer of Grim Fandango — critically acclaimed but commercially disappointing. Often ranked among the greatest games ever made.
- **2000:** Left LucasArts to found Double Fine Productions.
- **2005:** Released Psychonauts — critically acclaimed quirky platformer; commercially modest.
- **2009:** Released Brütal Legend (published by EA).
- **2012:** Launched Broken Age Kickstarter — raised $3.45M (goal was $400k), becoming the most-funded game Kickstarter at the time.
- **2019:** Microsoft acquired Double Fine as part of Xbox Game Studios expansion.
- **2021:** Released Psychonauts 2 — critical and commercial success, nominated for multiple Game Awards.
- **2023:** Double Fine PsychOdyssey — a 32-part, 30-hour documentary series covering the full Psychonauts 2 development (2014–2021). Landmark transparency in game development.

- **Adventure game royalty:** Schafer defined the LucasArts era of adventure games with his unique blend of humor, heart, and filmic ambition.
- **Kickstarter trailblazer:** The Broken Age Kickstarter proved that nostalgia-driven crowdfunding could support mid-budget games — for better or worse. It was a turning point in indie game financing.
- **Double Fine's culture:** Known for positive, creative-friendly work environment. PsychOdyssey documents a team that is passionate, overworked at times, but generally healthy compared to industry norms.

## Labour context

- Double Fine (under Schafer) is repeatedly cited as a positive workplace:
  - Reasonable hours, minimal crunch.
  - Creative autonomy for team members.
  - Transparent communication about budget and timelines.
  - PsychOdyssey documentary shows realistic but not exploitative development.
- Acquired by Microsoft in 2019 — the transition did not result in layoffs or cultural change.
- No union presence at Double Fine.
- The 2023–2024 industry layoff wave did not affect Double Fine.
- Schafer has spoken at GDC about the importance of team culture, work-life balance, and sustainable development (e.g., "Amnesia Fortnight" internal prototyping sessions).
