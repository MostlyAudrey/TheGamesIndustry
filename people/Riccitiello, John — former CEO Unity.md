# John Riccitiello

American business executive who served as CEO of Electronic Arts (2007–2013) and Unity Technologies (2014–2023). A controversial figure in the game industry — known for aggressive monetization at EA and the disastrous Runtime Fee at Unity that ended his tenure.

## Career arc

- **Early career:** Worked at Clorox, PepsiCo, Häagen-Dazs (Grand Metropolitan). President/CEO of Wilson Sporting Goods (1993–1995). President/CEO of Sara Lee Bakery Worldwide (1996–1997).
- **1997–2004:** Joined EA as president and COO.
- **2004:** Co-founded Elevation Partners (private equity) with Roger McNamee and U2's Bono.
- **2007–2013:** Returned to EA as CEO. EA won "Worst Company in America" in Consumerist poll two years running (2012, 2013). Infamously suggested "a dollar to reload" microtransactions in Battlefield.
- **2014–2023:** CEO of Unity Technologies. Led company to IPO (2020). Engine expanded beyond gaming.
- **2023:** Runtime Fee announcement (Sep) triggered developer revolt. Resigned (Oct).
- **2024:** Co-owner of Gratz Pilates.

## Key relationships

- Succeeded at EA by Andrew Wilson (current EA CEO)
- Succeeded at Unity by James Whitehurst (interim), then Matthew Bromberg
- Roelof Botha (Sequoia Capital) replaced him as Unity chairman
- Worked with Bobby Kotick (ActiBlizz CEO) — Kotick later called him "worst CEO in games"
- Legal dispute with Anne Evans (former Unity HR VP, sexual harassment lawsuit)

## Labor context

Riccitiello's tenure at both EA and Unity was marked by layoffs and monetization controversies that drew fire on labor conditions. The Runtime Fee directly caused the 1,800-person layoff at Unity in Jan 2024 under the "company reset."
