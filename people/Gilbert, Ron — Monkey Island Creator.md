# Ron Gilbert — Creator of Monkey Island

**The architect of the golden age of point-and-click adventure games.** Gilbert created SCUMM (Script Creation Utility for Maniac Mansion), the engine that powered LucasArts' classic adventure game era, and designed Maniac Mansion and the first two Monkey Island games.

## Career arc

- **1983:** While a student, co-wrote Graphics BASIC, an extension for the Commodore 64.
- **1983–1984:** Worked at HESware on action games. Company went bankrupt.
- **1985:** Hired at Lucasfilm Games (later LucasArts).
- **1987:** Invented the SCUMM scripting system. Designed Maniac Mansion — a non-linear adventure game that allowed players to choose from multiple characters.
- **1990:** Designed The Secret of Monkey Island — widely considered one of the greatest adventure games of all time.
- **1991:** Co-designed Monkey Island 2: LeChuck's Revenge.
- **1992:** Left LucasArts to co-found Humongous Entertainment with Shelley Day. Specialized in high-quality children's adventure games (Putt-Putt, Pajama Sam, Freddi Fish).
- **1995:** Co-founded Cavedog Entertainment (sister company to Humongous), producing the RTS classic Total Annihilation.
- **2001–2003:** Co-founded Hulabee Entertainment, another children's game studio.
- **2004–2007:** Worked with Beep Games.
- **2008–2010:** Creative director at Hothead Games (Vancouver). Also worked with Telltale Games and Penny Arcade.
- **2013:** Announced departure from Double Fine (where he had been working on The Cave).
- **2014–2017:** Founded Terrible Toybox. Developed Thimbleweed Park, a modern retro-style adventure game.
- **2022:** Returned to his most famous franchise — co-wrote and co-designed Return to Monkey Island, reuniting with original artist Steve Purcell and composer Michael Land.

- **SCUMM engine:** Gilbert's scripting language powered The Secret of Monkey Island, Maniac Mansion, Day of the Tentacle, Sam & Max, Full Throttle, and more — arguably the most important middleware in adventure game history.
- **Children's game pioneer:** Humongous Entertainment produced some of the most popular children's PC games of the 1990s, with strong educational and storytelling values.
- **The Monkey Island return:** Return to Monkey Island (2022) was a major event — Gilbert returned to the series after three decades, bringing his signature style to a modern audience.

## Labour context

- Gilbert has founded or co-founded multiple companies (Humongous, Cavedog, Hulabee, Terrible Toybox) — giving him direct experience as an employer and business owner.
- Humongous Entertainment is notable for its longevity (1992–2000s) and the stability provided by its children's-game niche.
- Gilbert has been publicly critical of the patent system and the way broad software patents affect game development — a position aligned with developer-friendly industry advocacy.
- No union context across any of Gilbert's studios. He represents an earlier era of the industry when labor organizing was effectively unheard of.
