# Tom Hall — Co-founder of id Software and Ion Storm

**One of the foundational designers of the first-person shooter genre.** Hall co-founded id Software and created Commander Keen, contributed to Wolfenstein 3D and DOOM, and later co-founded Ion Storm.

## Career arc

- **1980s:** Worked as a game developer in the early 1990s, part of the group of friends (Romero, Carmack) who would form id Software.
- **1990:** Co-founded id Software. Designed Commander Keen (a series of platformers that proved PC games could match console smooth-scrolling).
- **1991–1992:** Contributed to Wolfenstein 3D level design and game development.
- **1993:** Worked on DOOM, including level design and the DOOM Bible (design document). Left id before DOOM's release due to creative differences — notably, he wanted more story and exploration (including jumping), while Carmack and Romero favored pure action.
- **1996:** Co-founded Ion Storm with John Romero in Dallas, Texas.
- **1997–1999:** At Ion Storm, designed Anachronox — a sci-fi RPG released in 2000 to a mixed reception.
- **2001:** After Ion Storm's restructuring, co-founded Monkeystone Games with John Romero to develop mobile games.
- **2000s–2010s:** Continued consulting and working on various projects; less visible than his id/Ion Storm peers.
- **2020s:** Has occasionally commented on video game history, particularly the id Software years.

## Key games

| Game | Year | Role | Significance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Commander Keen | 1990–1991 | Lead designer | First major id game; PC smooth-scrolling breakthrough |
| Wolfenstein 3D | 1992 | Designer | Established first-person shooter genre |
| DOOM | 1993 | Designer (levels, DOOM Bible) | Revolutionized FPS gaming |
| Anachronox | 2000 | Lead designer | Idiosyncratic sci-fi RPG |
| Deus Ex | 2000 | Development involvement | At Ion Storm; he had active role |

- **The DOOM Bible:** Hall's design document for DOOM envisioned a more narrative-driven game with exploration and larger level concepts — many ideas that were cut (or deferred) were later incorporated into other games and mods.
- **Creativity over commerce:** Hall's design philosophy emphasized story, exploration, and world-building, in contrast to the pure-action approach that ultimately defined DOOM.

## Labour context

- Hall's career spans the garage-to-corporate transition of the game industry.
- id Software was famously non-hierarchical in its early days — four young men in a tiny office.
- Ion Storm represented the excess of the late-1990s: funded by Eidos, the studio had a lavish Dallas penthouse office, but its expensive failure (Daikatana) contributed to Ion Storm's closure.
- Monkeystone Games reflected the early mobile gaming boom (2001) — a small team targeting a nascent platform.
- No union presence at any of Hall's employers. The 1990s/2000s game industry had virtually no unionization.
