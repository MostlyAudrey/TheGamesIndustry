# Sundance DiGiovanni — Co-founder, Major League Gaming / ESL

**The founder of modern competitive gaming.** Sundance DiGiovanni co-founded Major League Gaming (MLG) in 2002, creating the first large-scale professional esports league in North America and laying the foundation for the modern esports industry.

## Career arc

- **2002:** Co-founded Major League Gaming with Mike Sepso in New York City.
- **2002–2005:** Built MLG from grassroots competitions into a structured league. Focused initially on Halo and Super Smash Bros. Melee.
- **2006:** Launched the Boost Mobile MLG Pro Circuit — television broadcasts of Halo 2 tournaments. First time esports reached a mass TV audience in North America.
- **2006–2010:** MLG expanded to include Call of Duty, Gears of War, and other shooters. Pro Circuit toured multiple US cities annually.
- **2011–2015:** MLG evolved into a media company, investing in streaming infrastructure, video production, and online platforms. Faced competition from newer esports organizations.
- **2016 (January):** Activision Blizzard acquired Major League Gaming for a reported ~$46M. DiGiovanni's role was absorbed into Activision's esports division.
- **2020s:** MLG's influence declined as Activision Blizzard developed its own esports properties (Overwatch League, Call of Duty League) separately.
- **2024 (January):** Activision Blizzard closed Major League Gaming's remaining operations.

## Key contributions

- **First esports league:** MLG established the template for professional video game competitions — structured seasons, professional production, player contracts, and mainstream sponsorships.
- **Esports broadcasting:** MLG's Pro Circuit television broadcasts were pioneers, bringing competitive gaming to ESPN and other networks.
- **Player livelihoods:** MLG was among the first to provide competitive players with salaries, travel support, and prize pools that approached professional sports levels.

## Labour context

- MLG was an independent esports organization operating with thin margins during most of its existence.
- The esports industry has chronic labour stability issues:
  - Player incomes are volatile, dependent on prize pools and sponsorship.
  - Production and operations staff face job precarity as leagues consolidate or collapse.
  - Esports organizations have high failure rates.
- DiGiovanni's MLG was eventually folded into Activision Blizzard — a pattern of independent esports being absorbed by publisher owners.
- The Overwatch League (founded 2017, effectively dissolved 2024) and Call of Duty League similarly failed to establish sustainable esports economics.
