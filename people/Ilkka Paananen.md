# Ilkka Paananen

**Role:** CEO & Co-founder, Supercell  
**Orgs:** [[Supercell]], [[Tencent]] (majority owner), [[Digital Chocolate]] (former), [[The LEGO Group]] (former board)

## Career Arc

Ilkka Paananen is a Finnish mobile gaming executive who co-founded Supercell in Helsinki in 2010. Before founding Supercell, he served as President of **Digital Chocolate**, an early social gaming pioneer founded by Electronic Arts founder Trip Hawkins. Under Paananen's leadership, Supercell became one of the most successful mobile game companies in history, with titles including *Clash of Clans*, *Hay Day*, *Boom Beach*, *Clash Royale*, and *Brawl Stars*.

Paananen raised over $143M from Accel, Index Ventures, Atomico, IVP, and others before Tencent acquired a majority stake at a reported $10.2B valuation. As of 2026, Paananen remains CEO of Supercell. He has also served on the board of The LEGO Group.

## Key Decisions

- **Pivot from browser to mobile (2011):** After the lukewarm reception of their browser game *Gunshine.net*, Paananen and the team pivoted hard to mobile, canceling projects and rebuilding for iOS/Android.
- **"Cell" structure:** Organized Supercell into small independent teams ("cells") with maximum autonomy — a structure that became legendary in game development.
- **Cultivating a "fail fast" culture:** Encouraged canceling games that didn't meet internal quality bars, even late in development.
- **Tencent partnership (2016):** Sold a majority stake to Tencent, giving Supercell operational independence while accessing Tencent's distribution in Asia.
- **Sustained live operations:** Invested deeply in long-term live service models rather than annual sequels.

## Labor Context

Paananen has been an outspoken advocate for developer autonomy and studio culture. Supercell's Helsinki studio is known for relatively flat hierarchies, high compensation, and a culture where teams self-organize. The company has been scrutinized in labor discussions around the Finnish gaming scene — particularly regarding crunch culture, though Supercell has generally been rated well compared to industry peers. Its acquisition by Tencent raised questions about Chinese ownership of Western studios, though Supercell maintains operational independence.
