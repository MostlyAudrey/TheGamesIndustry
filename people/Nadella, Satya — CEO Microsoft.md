# Satya Nadella — Chairman and CEO, Microsoft

**Chairman and CEO of Microsoft since 2014/2021.** The most powerful executive in technology, Nadella transformed Microsoft from a Windows-centric company to a cloud-first, platform-agnostic giant. His gaming legacy is defined by the two largest acquisitions in industry history: ZeniMax/Bethesda ($7.5B) and Activision Blizzard ($68.7B), which made Microsoft Gaming the third-largest game company by revenue.

## Career arc

- **1992:** Joined Microsoft from Sun Microsystems — early work on Windows NT and server products.
- **2000s:** Rose through server and tools division; became EVP of Cloud and Enterprise in 2011.
- **2014:** Named CEO — shifted Microsoft from "Windows first" to "mobile first, cloud first" strategy. Azure became a central pillar.
- **2021:** Approved ZeniMax acquisition — brought Bethesda Game Studios, id Software, Arkane under Xbox.
- **2022:** Approved the landmark Activision Blizzard acquisition — the defining deal of his tenure.
- **2023-10:** Closed Activision Blizzard acquisition after overcoming regulatory pushback from FTC, CMA (UK), and EC.
- **2024-2026:** Presided over significant gaming layoffs totaling ~5,100+ as Microsoft Gaming integrated ABK.

## Strategic approach to gaming

Nadella has described gaming as Microsoft's "consumer flywheel" — a way to reach billions of players, particularly on mobile (via King/Candy Crush). Unlike Ballmer-era Xbox, which treated gaming as a standalone console business, Nadella's Microsoft sees gaming as a subscription/content business layered on Azure cloud infrastructure.

Key strategic pillars under Nadella:
1. **Game Pass** — subscription model as the center of gaming strategy
2. **xCloud** — cloud gaming powered by Azure data centers
3. **Cross-platform** — releasing first-party games on PlayStation and Nintendo (a reversal of previous strategy)
4. **Scale through acquisition** — ZeniMax and ABK as the primary growth vehicles

## Labor context

Nadella delegated gaming labor relations to Phil Spencer and the Microsoft Gaming leadership. However, as CEO, he:

- Approved the labor neutrality agreement that CWA negotiated with Microsoft in 2022
- Oversaw Microsoft's overall corporate posture toward unions (neutral/accepting) which differed from many Big Tech peers
- Approved massive layoffs in gaming (2024 and 2026) that ultimately included union members once the neutrality agreement expired

The tension between Microsoft's public labor posture and its headcount reductions has been a defining feature of his second term.
