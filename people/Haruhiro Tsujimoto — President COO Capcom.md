# Haruhiro Tsujimoto

Haruhiro Tsujimoto (born October 19, 1964) is the President, Representative Director, and COO of [[orgs/Capcom]]. He has held the position since July 2007, making him one of the longest-serving leaders in the Japanese game industry.

## Career Arc

- Joined Capcom in April 1987 — early career within the company
- Became Director of the Company in June 1997
- Managing Director (February 1999), Senior Managing Director (April 2001)
- Director and Executive Corporate Officer (July 2004)
- Director and Executive Vice President (April 2006)
- Appointed President and Representative Director, COO in July 2007
- Has overseen Capcom's renaissance through the 2010s with the success of Resident Evil (7, 2 Remake, Village), Monster Hunter World, and Devil May Cry 5

## Key Decisions

- Led Capcom through its early 2010s struggles and back to sustained profitability
- Approached game development with a focus on proprietary engine technology (RE Engine)
- Shifted Capcom's strategy toward simultaneous global releases and multiplatform launches
- Oversaw the revival of classic franchises through remakes (Resident Evil 2, 3, 4)
- Navigated the company through the COVID-19 pandemic and digital transition
- Separated CEO and COO roles: Kenzo Tsujimoto (his father) served as Chairman with ultimate authority

## Labor Context

- Capcom has faced controversy over allegedly "exploitative" treatment of e-sports competitors
- Capcom was investigated for "karoshi" overwork concerns in 2021 but denied allegations
- No direct personal labor controversies
- Company has maintained mostly positive employee relations compared to industry peers
