# William Ding (Ding Lei)

William Lei Ding (born October 1, 1971 in Fenghua, Ningbo, Zhejiang, China) is the founder and CEO of [[orgs/NetEase]], one of China's largest internet and gaming companies.

## Career Arc

- Educated at the University of Electronic Science and Technology of China
- Worked at telecommunications companies before starting NetEase
- Founded NetEase in 1997, originally as an internet portal and email service
- Led the company through the dot-com bubble; NetEase was near bankruptcy in 2000-2001
- Shifted strategy heavily toward online gaming in the early 2000s, which became the company's primary revenue driver
- Director since July 1999; CEO since November 2005
- NetEase became a major player through partnerships with Blizzard Entertainment (World of Warcraft in China, 2008-2023) and self-developed titles like Fantasy Westward Journey and Identity V
- One of the wealthiest individuals in China; net worth estimated at $38.9 billion (2025)

## Key Decisions in Gaming

- Secured exclusive rights to publish Blizzard games in China (2008) — a landmark deal worth billions
- Invested heavily in in-house mobile game development leading to major successes
- Lost Blizzard partnership in 2023 after licensing dispute; deal was revived in 2024
- Expanded globally through studios in Japan (Nagoshi Studio, Grasshopper Manufacture, GPTRACK50), North America, and Europe
- Acquired and established multiple overseas studios including Tango Gameworks (2024)

## Labor Context

- NetEase faced criticism in 2021 over mandatory overtime ("996" culture)
- Chinese game industry generally lacks independent unions
- NetEase has been involved in industry-wide Chinese government crackdowns on gaming (2021)
- No major labor scandals unique to Ding's leadership
