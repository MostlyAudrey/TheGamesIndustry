# Feargus Urquhart — CEO, Obsidian Entertainment

**The CEO who kept Obsidian alive through two decades of industry turbulence.** Urquhart founded Black Isle Studios, produced Baldur's Gate and the original Fallout, and founded Obsidian Entertainment, leading it from near-collapse to Microsoft acquisition.

## Career arc

- **1990s:** Worked at Interplay Entertainment, rising through the ranks during the company's golden age of RPG development.
- **1997:** Founded and led Black Isle Studios, Interplay's internal RPG division. Oversaw development of Fallout, Fallout 2, Planescape: Torment, Icewind Dale, and the Baldur's Gate series.
- **2003:** When Interplay encountered financial difficulties and Black Isle's future became uncertain, Urquhart led a group of veteran developers to found Obsidian Entertainment in Irvine, California.
- **2004–2012:** Obsidian operated on a work-for-hire model, developing sequels for major publishers: Star Wars: Knights of the Old Republic II, Neverwinter Nights 2, Fallout: New Vegas, Dungeon Siege III, South Park: The Stick of Truth.
- **2012:** Facing financial pressure, Obsidian launched a Kickstarter for Pillars of Eternity — raised $3.99M, reviving the classic isometric RPG genre.
- **2015:** Pillars of Eternity critically and commercially successful.
- **2018:** Microsoft acquired Obsidian as part of Xbox Game Studios expansion. Urquhart remained CEO.
- **2019:** Released The Outer Worlds — Obsidian's first major new IP.
- **2020–2026:** Multiple projects under Microsoft: Grounded, Pentiment, Avowed, The Outer Worlds 2.

- **Obsidian's survival story:** The studio nearly closed multiple times (2007, 2012) between publisher contract gaps and cancelled projects.
- **Work-for-hire model:** For its first decade, Obsidian was a "gun for hire" — building sequels to others' IPs. This created financial instability, as the studio bore development cost but owned no IP.
- **Kickstarter revival:** Urquhart's decision to turn to Kickstarter for Pillars of Eternity was a pivotal moment, proving that classic-style RPGs had a viable market.

## Labour context

- Obsidian has maintained a more stable culture than many peers, though pre-acquisition financial precarity meant periods of uncertainty.
- Urquhart has been publicly transparent about Obsidian's financial struggles.
- Under Obsidian's profit-sharing model, employees received bonuses tied to game performance.
- Post-acquisition by Microsoft, Obsidian has grown substantially with stable funding.
- No union presence at Obsidian.
- The studio was not significantly affected by the 2023–2024 industry layoffs.
