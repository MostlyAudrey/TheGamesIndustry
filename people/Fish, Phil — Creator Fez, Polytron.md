# Phil Fish — Creator of Fez

**The brilliant, tragic figure of the indie game boom.** Fish's Fez (2012) is a 2D platformer with a dimension-rotating mechanic that became one of the most acclaimed indie games of its era. His public exit from game development after sustained harassment made him a symbol of the industry's toxicity toward creators.

## Career arc

- **Early career:** Born in Quebec. Studied game design at the Montreal National Animation and Design Centre. Worked at Ubisoft and Artificial Mind & Movement.
- **2005:** Co-founded Kokoromi, a game-art collective organizing Montreal's annual GAMMA events.
- **2007 (announced):** Fez revealed to the public. Development dragged on for five years.
- **2008:** Founded Polytron Corporation to develop Fez.
- **2012 (April):** Fez launched on Xbox Live Arcade — widespread critical acclaim. Known for its folding 3D/2D mechanic, retro aesthetic, and intricate puzzle design.
- **2012:** Fish appeared prominently in Indie Game: The Movie alongside Jonathan Blow and Edmund McMillen. The film depicted the stress of Fez's development and Fish's difficult relationship with business partner Renaud Bédard.
- **2013:** Following an online argument at a public panel, Fish was subjected to sustained doxxing and harassment. He announced he was leaving game development.
- **2014–2015:** Fish partially returned but ultimately exited from active game development. Polytron published Panoramical (2015) without Fish's direct involvement.
- **2016:** Kokoromi released SuperHyperCube — a VR game originally prototyped during Fez's development.
- **2017–present:** Fish has made sporadic public appearances and statements but has not released a new game. Fez remains his only shipped title.

- **Fez's design legacy:** The dimension-rotating mechanic — switching between 2D views of a 3D world — was genuinely novel. Fez influenced countless later games (Monument Valley, etc.).
- **Crunch and development hell:** Fez took five years and nearly collapsed multiple times. The documentary showed the toll this took on Fish personally.
- **The "toxic fandom" moment:** Fish's experience of doxxing and harassment after a single public blow-up became a case study in how game developers are treated online.
- **Industry departure:** Fish's multiple public exits and eventual silence became a sobering coda to the "indie game dream" narrative of the 2010s.

## Labour context

- Fish worked at major publishers (Ubisoft) before going independent — giving him direct experience of corporate game development.
- The Fez development cycle (2007–2012) involved significant personal strain, including financial precarity and creative pressure.
- Fish has been private about union or labour organizing views specifically, but his story is frequently invoked in discussions of:
  - Mental health in game development
  - Developer harassment and abuse
  - The unsustainable nature of small-team, multi-year development
  - The mismatch between critical acclaim and financial stability for indie developers
- No union presence at Polytron.
