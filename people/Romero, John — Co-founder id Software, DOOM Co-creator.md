# John Romero — DOOM Co-creator, FPS Pioneer

**The most famous game designer of the 1990s — and the public face of the first-person shooter revolution.** Romero co-founded id Software and co-created Wolfenstein 3D, DOOM, and Quake — three of the most important games in the history of the medium.

## Career arc

- **1980s:** Started in the Apple II game development scene. Self-taught programmer and designer.
- **1991:** Co-founded id Software with John Carmack, Tom Hall, and Adrian Carmack. Released Commander Keen.
- **1992:** Wolfenstein 3D — established the first-person shooter genre.
- **1993:** DOOM — cultural phenomenon. Romero designed levels, coined "deathmatch," and created the multiplayer FPS culture. DOOM was responsible for popularizing online multiplayer gaming.
- **1994:** DOOM II — cemented franchise dominance.
- **1995:** Hexen — darker fantasy FPS.
- **1996:** Quake — true 3D FPS with advanced online multiplayer. Romero and Carmack's creative differences came to a head; Romero left id after Quake shipped.
- **1996–2001:** Co-founded Ion Storm (Dallas). Directed Daikatana — famously announced with the boast "John Romero's about to make you his bitch." Daikatana was delayed, over-budget, and critically panned on release.
- **2001:** Departed Ion Storm. Co-founded Monkeystone Games with Tom Hall for mobile development.
- **2003–2005:** Joined Midway Games as project lead on Gauntlet: Seven Sorrows.
- **2005:** Founded Gazillion Entertainment (later closed).
- **2016:** Announced Blackroom (FPS, crowd-funded concept) — cancelled.
- **2017–present:** Works on smaller projects, gives talks, promotes game development education and retro gaming. Has released DOOM-inspired level packs and consulted on DOOM (2016) and DOOM Eternal.

- **"Deathmatch" coiner:** Romero introduced the concept of player-vs-player multiplayer deathmatch in DOOM, shaping online gaming culture.
- **The Daikatana lesson:** One of gaming's most famous failures — a cautionary tale of hype, hubris, and the difficulty of managing a large team. Romero's public bravado backfired spectacularly.
- **The id Tech legacy:** The engine technology built by Romero and Carmack (id Tech 0–2) formed the basis of PC gaming's FPS revolution and was licensed extensively.
- **Indie game elder statesman:** In recent years, Romero has become a beloved figure in the retro-gaming scene and speaks frequently about game development history.

## Labour context

- Early id Software: 4-person team, no hierarchy, no HR, no formal processes. Extreme hours driven by passion rather than management pressure.
- Ion Storm: Large studio (~60+ people), lavishly funded. Crunch on Daikatana was extreme — the game was delayed from 1997 to 2000.
- Post-Ion Storm: Smaller studios (Monkeystone, Gazillion, Midway) — conventional work environments.
- Romero has in recent years spoken more openly about the importance of healthy team dynamics, sustainable development, and the damage done by crunch culture.
- No union presence at any of Romero's studios. He worked in an era before game unionization existed.
