# Dennis Crowley

**Role:** Co-founder & Executive Chairman, Foursquare  
**Orgs:** [[Foursquare]], [[Google]] (acquired Dodgeball), [[NYU]] (faculty)

## Career Arc

Dennis Crowley (born June 19, 1976) is an American Internet entrepreneur who pioneered location-based social networking. He co-founded **Dodgeball**, an early location-based social network that Google acquired in 2005. After Google shut down Dodgeball, Crowley co-founded **Foursquare** in 2009, which popularized check-in mechanics, gamified location sharing, and venue discovery.

Foursquare evolved from a consumer app into a location-intelligence platform powering products used by 50M+ people monthly across 100 countries. Crowley served as CEO for years before becoming Executive Chairman. His work on gamified check-ins — earning badges, mayorships, and points — directly anticipated the reward-loop mechanics that became standard in mobile/social gaming.

## Key Decisions

- **Created Dodgeball (2000):** One of the first location-based social networks, sold to Google.
- **Founded Foursquare (2009):** Gamified check-ins with badges, mayorships, and points — influential on later mobile game engagement design.
- **Pivoted Foursquare to location intelligence (2014):** Split the app into Foursquare (check-ins) and Swarm (gamified sharing), while building a B2B data business.
- **Stayed independent:** Refused multiple acquisition offers, maintaining Foursquare as an independent company.

## Labor Context

Crowley has been a prominent voice in NYC tech culture and mentored many founders. Foursquare's evolution from consumer darling to B2B platform required significant workforce restructuring, including layoffs as the company shifted strategy. Crowley's approach to balancing product vision with business realities is studied in startup circles.
