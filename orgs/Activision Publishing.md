# Activision Publishing

**One of the largest third-party video game publishers in the world; now a subsidiary of Microsoft Gaming.** Originally founded in 1979 as the first independent console game developer. Acquired by Bobby Kotick-led investors in 1991, restructured, and grew through acquisitions to become the powerhouse behind *Call of Duty*, *Guitar Hero*, *Spyro*, *Crash Bandicoot*, and *Tony Hawk*.

Activision Publishing is the operating subsidiary of Activision Blizzard (holding company) that develops and publishes all games except those from [[orgs/Blizzard Entertainment]]. Rob Kostich has been president since 2019.

## Pre-Microsoft labor history

### Call of Duty crunch culture
Activision's flagship Call of Duty franchise has a well-documented history of severe crunch across its rotating development studios. A landmark 2019 report from [[sources/2019-04-20 — kotaku-human-cost-call-of-duty-black-ops-4]] described Treyarch developers working 100-hour weeks during the Black Ops 4 cycle, with QA testers reporting "sweatshop" conditions ([[sources/2019-04-20 — pcgamer-treyarch-sweatshop-qa]]). The pattern repeated across Infinity Ward and Sledgehammer Games, driven by the annualized Call of Duty release schedule that Activision enforced.

### ABK DFEH lawsuit and employee walkout (2021)
On July 20, 2021, the California Department of Fair Employment and Housing (DFEH) filed a lawsuit against Activision Blizzard alleging "frat boy" culture of systemic sexual harassment, discrimination, and unequal pay ([[sources/2021-07-21 — bloomberg-dfeh-activision-blizzard-lawsuit]]; [[sources/2021-07-20 — npr-activision-blizzard-lawsuit-dfeh]]). Over 2,600 employees signed an open letter demanding the company take the allegations seriously. On July 28, thousands walked out in protest, organized by A Better ABK ([[sources/2021-07-28 — washpost-blizzard-employee-walkout]]). The lawsuit was settled for $54 million in December 2023 with no admission of widespread harassment.

### Raven Software QA strike (2021-2022)
In December 2021, Activision laid off ~12 of 40 QA testers at Raven Software, a Call of Duty support studio. The remaining QA workers walked out, forming Game Workers Alliance-CWA. The union won an NLRB election 19-2 in May 2022 — the **first union at an Activision Blizzard studio** ([[sources/2022-05-23 — techcrunch-raven-software-union-wins]]). This directly set the precedent for the Microsoft-CWA Labor Neutrality Agreement and the wave of organizing that followed.

### Activision QA United-CWA formation (2024)
Under the Microsoft neutrality agreement, Activision QA testers across California, Texas, and Minnesota organized as [[orgs/Activision QA United-CWA]] — the largest certified union in US game history at formation (~600 members). Microsoft voluntarily recognized the unit in March 2024 ([[sources/2024-03-11 — gamedeveloper-activision-600-qa-union]]).

## Post-acquisition (2023-present)

### Restructuring and layoffs
- **January 2024:** Microsoft Gaming laid off 1,900 workers (~9% of gaming division), disproportionately impacting Activision Blizzard. CWA-represented units were exempted ([[sources/2024-01-25 — cnbc-microsoft-1900-layoffs]]; [[sources/2024-01-25 — cwa-microsoft-1900-layoffs-statement]]).
- **2024-2026:** Activision QA workforce declined from 600 to 400 through attrition without replacement while workload increased, reflecting a quiet headcount reduction ([[sources/2026-10-16 — playerone-activision-qa-strike-authorization]]).
- **July 2026:** Microsoft announced an additional 3,200 gaming layoffs, but these primarily impacted ZeniMax/Bethesda rather than Activision ([[sources/2026-07-06 — aftermath-microsoft-unions-battle]]).

### Studio expansion
- **May 2024:** Activision established Elsewhere Entertainment in Warsaw, Poland — a new AAA studio tasked with creating a narrative-driven original IP, not a Call of Duty title. The studio was assembled from talent across the industry.

### Halo transfer (September 2026)
In a major restructuring, Microsoft transferred the Halo franchise, World's Edge, and Rare from Xbox Game Studios to Activision. Activision is now responsible for developing the next Halo game ([[sources/2026-09-01 — ign-halo-moves-to-activision]]). This move signals consolidation of major IPs under Activision's development engine.

### Contract crisis: Activision QA United (2026)
Despite the Blizzard contract win in September 2026, [[orgs/Activision QA United-CWA]] still had no first contract after 2.5 years of bargaining. The workforce had shrunk from 600 to 400. On October 16, 2026, the union voted to authorize a strike in the Modern Warfare 4 launch window ([[sources/2026-10-16 — playerone-activision-qa-strike-authorization]]). Key issues: wages, staffing, AI automation, and contractor use. Next bargaining scheduled Oct 21-22.

### Raven Software contract (August 2025)
The Activision union success story: Raven QA workers ratified their first contract in August 2025 after three years of bargaining ([[sources/2025-08-04 — cwa-raven-software-first-contract]]). The contract included wage increases, recall rights, and severance provisions — making them the first Activision union to secure a contract.

## Current position within Microsoft Gaming

Activision Publishing operates as a subsidiary of Activision Blizzard (the holding company), which sits under [[orgs/Microsoft Gaming]] alongside Xbox Game Studios and ZeniMax Media/Bethesda. Since the Halo transfer, Activision has expanded beyond its core Call of Duty portfolio. The division manages development across 10+ internal studios with the majority focused on CoD.

Key structural notes:
- Activision and Blizzard remain separate operating units under the Activision Blizzard holding company
- Activision publishes Call of Duty on annual cycle (Infinity Ward/Treyarch/Sledgehammer rotation) plus Warzone live service
- The Sep 2026 Halo transfer expanded Activision's franchise portfolio beyond its traditional scope
- Unlike Blizzard, no wall-to-wall unions have formed at any Activision studio — only QA units are organized
