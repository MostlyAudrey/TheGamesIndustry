# Meta (Platforms) Games

> The video game and virtual reality operations of Meta Platforms, Inc., primarily through its Reality Labs division. HQ: Menlo Park, CA.

Meta's gaming presence is concentrated in its Reality Labs division, which develops the Quest VR headset line, the Horizon Worlds social VR platform, and operates Oculus Studios (first-party VR game publishing). Since late 2020, Reality Labs has accumulated **over $70 billion in cumulative losses** ([[sources/2026-01-13 — cnbc-meta-reality-labs-1000-layoffs-studios]]).

## Mass layoffs 2022-2023

### November 2022 — 11,000 layoffs

Meta announced its first-ever mass layoff in November 2022, cutting 11,000 jobs — 13% of its workforce. The layoffs affected all divisions, including Reality Labs' VR and metaverse teams. CEO Mark Zuckerberg took responsibility for over-hiring during the pandemic boom ([[sources/2022-11-09 — pcgamer-meta-11000-layoffs-reality-labs]]).

### March 2023 — 10,000 layoffs

Meta's second mass layoff cut an additional 10,000 jobs and eliminated 5,000 open job postings. Zuckerberg declared 2023 the "Year of Efficiency." Reality Labs was again impacted ([[sources/2023-03-14 — cnbc-meta-10000-layoffs]]).

## VR studio closures and ongoing cuts (2024-2026)

### Ready at Dawn (closed August 2024)

Developer of the Lone Echo series and Echo VR. Meta acquired the studio in 2020. It was the first high-profile VR studio closure as Meta began consolidating its game development operations ([[sources/2024-08-08 — verge-meta-ready-at-dawn-closure]]).

### Downpour Interactive (closed 2025)

Developer of the VR military simulation game Onward. Acquired by Meta in 2021. Closed as Meta continued to reduce its VR game development footprint ([[sources/2026-01-14 — gamedeveloper-meta-vr-studios-shutdown-overview]]).

### Reality Labs 10% layoff + 3 studio closures (January 2026)

The most significant restructuring event for Meta's gaming operations. **Approximately 1,000 jobs** eliminated (10% of Reality Labs). Three VR game development studios were completely shut down:

- **Armature Studio** — ported Resident Evil 4 to VR; acquired 2022
- **Twisted Pixel Games** — developed Marvel's Deadpool VR; acquired 2022
- **Sanzaru Games** — developed Asgard's Wrath franchise; acquired 2020

Additionally, **Oculus Studios Central Technology unit** was eliminated, **Ouro Interactive** (first-party Horizon Worlds studio) was cut, and the **Supernatural** VR fitness app (acquired for $400M+) was placed into maintenance mode with no new content.

The stated reason was Meta's shift from VR/metaverse investments toward **wearables** (Ray-Ban Meta smart glasses) and **AI** ([[sources/2026-01-13 — cnbc-meta-reality-labs-1000-layoffs-studios]]; [[sources/2026-01-13 — verge-meta-closes-three-vr-studios]]).

### March 2026 — Hundreds more cuts

Hundreds of additional employees were laid off, with Reality Labs again a prime recipient ([[sources/2026-01-14 — gamedeveloper-meta-vr-studios-shutdown-overview]]).

## Union activity

**No known union organizing** at Meta or any of its gaming/VR studios. Meta has a documented history of aggressive anti-union activity and has not experienced any successful unionization efforts across its US operations as of 2026.

## Strategic direction

Meta's gaming strategy has undergone a dramatic reversal:

- **2014-2020:** Build the VR ecosystem through Oculus acquisition and studio purchases
- **2020-2022:** Metaverse bet — $70B+ invested in Reality Labs
- **2023-2025:** Year of Efficiency cuts + gradual pivot toward AI
- **2026:** Reality Labs reoriented from VR gaming to wearables; Horizon Worlds pivoting to mobile/Roblox-style experiences; AI gets the investment that metaverse once had

In January 2026, Meta CTO Andrew Bosworth stated Reality Labs was shifting investment from metaverse toward wearables ([[sources/2026-01-13 — verge-meta-closes-three-vr-studios]]). Key AI hires included Scale AI founder Alexandr Wang for $14.3B in June 2025 ([[sources/2026-01-13 — cnbc-meta-reality-labs-1000-layoffs-studios]]).
