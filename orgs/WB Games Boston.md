## History

**Turbine era (1994–2018):** Founded as CyberSpace by Brown University AI Lab students. Changed to Turbine in 1995. Pioneered early 3D MMORPGs with Asheron's Call (1999), the third 3D MMO ever after Meridian 59 and EverQuest.

**Warner acquisition (2010):** Warner Bros. purchased Turbine for $160M in April 2010. The MMOs continued operating.

**MMO spin-off (2016):** Warner shifted Turbine's focus to mobile games. LOTRO and DDO development was spun off to Standing Stone Games (formed by former Turbine staff), published by Daybreak Game Company. Asheron's Call servers shut down January 2017.

**Rebranding to WB Games Boston (2018):** The studio was rebranded in November 2018.

**MultiVersus era:** WB Games Boston provided support for MultiVersus (developed by Player First Games). Following MultiVersus's commercial failure and Player First's closure in February 2025, WB Games Boston's role shifted.

## Current Focus

- Free-to-play mobile game development (as per WB Careers page)
- MultiVersus post-launch support (until shutdown)
- The studio has shifted significantly from MMO development to mobile

## Financial

- WB Games Boston is the smallest of the remaining WB studios
- The Turbine acquisition ($160M) was one of WB's largest studio purchases
- The MMO spin-off to Standing Stone Games suggests the studio was seen as non-core
