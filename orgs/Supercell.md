# Supercell

**Finnish mobile game developer and publisher**, HQ **Helsinki, Finland**. Founded **2010** by Ilkka Paananen and a team of six. The world's most profitable mobile games company by revenue per employee, known for developing a small number of massively successful live-service games. Majority-owned by **Tencent** through the **Halti S.A.** consortium (51.2% as of 2023).

## Capital structure

- **Private company** controlled through **Halti S.A.** (Luxembourg consortium)
- **Tencent** holds 51.2% of Halti S.A. (effective majority control)
- Original consortium (2016): Tencent-led group acquired ~84% from SoftBank for ~$10.2B
- Supercell's **founders and employees** retain a minority equity stake
- **Revenue:** $3B (2025), down 4% from record $3B+ (2024) — profits grew despite revenue dip (as of 2026-02-10, [[sources/2026-02-10 — gamesbeat-supercell-2025-results]])
- **2024 revenue:** ~$3B (record), up 77% from ~$1.7B (2023) driven by Brawl Stars rebound and Squad Busters launch (as of 2025-02, TechNode)

## Tencent relationship

Tencent is the de facto controlling shareholder but has maintained a "hands-off" approach to Supercell's operations and game development, consistent with Tencent's strategy for its major Western gaming investments (Riot Games, Epic Games). No reports of Tencent-imposed layoffs or restructuring directives. However, as the mobile gaming market grows more competitive globally, Tencent's financial discipline may increasingly influence Supercell's cost management.

## Key labor events (2022-2026)

### No direct layoffs until 2025-2026
Supercell maintained a long-standing reputation for avoiding layoffs, attributed to its small-team structure (each game team operates autonomously), high per-game revenue, and Finnish labor protections. The company had not conducted documented company-wide layoffs before the 2025-2026 period.

### North American division cuts (end 2025 – June 2026)
Beginning in late 2025, Supercell quietly reduced headcount in its North American division. In June 2026, MobileGamer reported layoffs affecting key staff. Steve Desilets, co-founder of Supercell's US studio, announced on LinkedIn that his team's game project had been canceled and the team disbanded. The extent of the cuts remains undisclosed by Supercell, which did not comment publicly.

### Metacore acquisition and 159 layoffs (May–June 2026)
This is the most significant labor event linked to Supercell. In May 2026, Supercell announced it would fully acquire Metacore (Merge Mansion developer), in which it was already a major shareholder having invested €180M. Simultaneously, Metacore announced restructuring:
- **Up to 160 roles** proposed for elimination in Finland (out of ~280 total employees)
- **Germany and Sweden offices** closed completely
- **Confirmed June 2026:** 159 job cuts in Finland were implemented

### Project cancellations
- **2022:** Supercell canceled *Clash Quest* and *Everdale* during development — impacted teams were reassigned, no documented layoffs
- **2026:** US project canceled, leading to US team disbandment and Desilets departure

## Union organizing

**No known union organizing at Supercell.** Key contextual factors:

1. **Finnish labor system:** Finland uses sector-level collective bargaining rather than company-level unions. Supercell employees are covered by the technology industry collective agreement, which sets minimum terms for wages, hours, and conditions. This may reduce the impetus for company-specific union formation.

2. **High compensation:** Supercell is known for paying significantly above Finnish tech industry averages, with profit-sharing bonuses. Glassdoor reports average salary of ~$67,112 globally, with Northern European median of ~$70,000.

3. **Company culture:** CEO Ilkka Paananen has built a public reputation for trust-based, autonomous team culture with strong work-life balance. However, Glassdoor reviews (2026) note burnout issues and a "boys club" culture.

4. **No US-style organizing:** Unlike Microsoft/Activision (CWA), Sega (AEGIS-CWA), or other Western studios, Supercell has faced no public unionization campaigns, elections, or voluntary recognition efforts at any of its global offices.

**Labor leverage assessment:** Supercell's high revenue per employee (~$1.25M/employee in 2024) and gross profit margins make it one of the most profitable game companies in the world. The absence of unionization despite Finland's union-dense labor market is notable — potentially attributable to high individual compensation, small autonomous team structure, and sector-level collective bargaining coverage that reduces transactional union demand.

## Employee sentiment

Revelio Labs (2026) reports overall employee sentiment at Supercell as **neutral and declining year-over-year**. Active job posting declines (15.4% in 2026 vs 2025) and reduced hiring velocity suggest cooling labor demand after the 2024 hiring peak. Glassdoor reviews mention burnout as a recurring issue, with staff "frequently going on sick leave or leaving due to burnouts" and a reputation for protecting leadership.
