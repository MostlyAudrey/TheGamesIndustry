# Tencent Holdings Ltd. — Games Power Map

## Overview

Tencent is the world's largest gaming company by revenue. Founded in 1998 by Pony Ma, it has grown from a Chinese instant messaging company into a diversified technology and entertainment conglomerate with a market cap exceeding $1 trillion. Its gaming division generated over $10 billion in overseas revenue alone in 2025.

Tencent's gaming strategy has been to acquire minority or majority stakes in leading studios worldwide while maintaining a hands-off operational approach, providing capital, distribution (especially into China via WeChat/QQ), and technical infrastructure.

## Labor Profile

### China Operations

Tencent's Chinese game development operations (TiMi, Lightspeed, Aurora, Morefun, Next) operate under China's state-controlled labor system. Independent unions are illegal — only the ACFTU (All-China Federation of Trade Unions) is permitted. Tencent likely maintains an ACFTU enterprise union as required by Chinese law. There are no documented cases of independent worker organizing at Tencent China.

In January 2022, a Tencent employee working on WeChat publicly challenged their manager for rewarding overwork behavior, highlighting the prevalence of "996" culture (9AM-9PM, 6 days/week) even at China's most prestigious tech employer.

### Subsidiary Labor Events

Tencent's wholly owned Western subsidiaries have experienced significant layoffs as part of industry-wide cost-cutting:

- **Riot Games (wholly owned)**: 530 layoffs in Jan 2024 (11% of workforce); additional 46 layoffs Jan 2023; 32 layoffs Oct 2024; 80 layoffs Feb 2026 (2XKO team); 12 publishing layoffs Feb 2026. Riot has a history of labor disputes including a 2019 employee walkout over sexual discrimination lawsuit handling and a $100M class action settlement (2021, approved 2023).

- **Sumo Digital (wholly owned)**: 15% workforce reduction in 2024; Secret Mode and The Chinese Room spun off as independent in 2025.

### Ubisoft/Tencent Labor Connection

Tencent's 2025 Vantage Studios investment coincided with Ubisoft's most intense period of labor organizing, including STJV strikes in France (700+ workers Oct 2024, Oct 2025, 1,200 across 5 unions Feb 2026), union certification at Ubisoft Halifax (Dec 2025, closed Jan 2026), and ongoing organizing at Ubisoft Montreal.

## Regulatory & Geopolitical Exposure

Tencent faces significant regulatory headwinds:
1. **US Military Blacklist** (Jan 2025) — DoD labeled Tencent a "Chinese military business"
2. **DOJ antitrust** (Feb 2025) — forced to remove Epic Games directors
3. **Epic stake pressure** (2026) — Trump camp reportedly pushing Tencent to sell Epic shares on national security grounds
4. **China tech crackdown** (2021-2022) — state media attacks on gaming; reduced gaming license approvals
5. **TikTok v. Garland precedent** — legal framework that could extend to Tencent
