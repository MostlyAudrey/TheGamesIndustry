# Nintendo Entertainment Planning & Development (EPD)

**Nintendo's largest internal software development division.** Formed September 16, 2015 from the merger of Entertainment Analysis & Development (EAD) and Software Planning & Development (SPD) — two divisions that had each existed for decades under Shigeru Miyamoto's leadership. The merger was part of a company-wide restructure under then-president Tatsumi Kimishima.

EPD consolidates all of Nintendo's internal game development, mobile app development, and external production management under one umbrella. It is organized into ~10 production groups plus centralized programming, design, and sound groups, each focusing on specific franchises or types of games. The Co-Production Group handles all externally developed Nintendo-published titles (e.g. Kirby, Pokémon, Fire Emblem, Xenoblade, and Mario & Luigi series).

## Key facts

- **~3,000+ employees** across Kyoto and Tokyo offices (exact figure not publicly broken out; EPD is the largest single division within Nintendo's ~7,317 consolidated employees as of March 2025)
- **No mass layoffs ever** — part of Nintendo's broader Japanese labor model where regular employees effectively have lifetime employment
- **No union** at any Nintendo Japan entity as of October 2026
- **Leadership:** Shinya Takahashi (Executive General Manager), Yoshiaki Koizumi (Senior General Manager), Katsuya Eguchi, Hisashi Nogami (Deputy GMs), with Eiji Aonuma, Takashi Tezuka, Yoshio Sakamoto, and Koji Kondo as senior officers
- **EPD output accounted for ~70% of Nintendo's software revenue during FY2024 and FY2025**, driving the Switch 2 transition
- **Annual salary increases** — Nintendo raised base wages across the company in 2022 and 2023, including EPD staff
- **Low turnover:** 1.9% in Japan, 5.1% at NOA; average tenure 14.4 years in Japan

## Labor note

EPD benefits from Japanese labor law that makes layoffs of regular employees nearly impossible unless a company faces existential financial distress. The four requirements under Japanese case law (the *four criteria*) are: (1) necessity of headcount reduction due to business necessity, (2) duty to avoid dismissal (transfers, voluntary retirement, etc.), (3) reasonableness of employee selection, and (4) proper procedures (consultation with labor unions or representatives). Nintendo has not faced any situation requiring layoffs given its consistent profitability.

This stands in stark contrast to the US game industry, where mass layoffs have become routine in the 2022-2026 period.

## Key decisions

- 2015-09-16: Division created from EAD + SPD merger under Kimishima restructure
- 2017-present: Delivered Switch launch titles and sustained first-party output throughout Switch lifecycle
- 2022-2023: Base wage increases applied across EPD and wider Nintendo
- 2023-2025: EPD drove ~70% of Nintendo software revenue, supporting Switch 2 transition
