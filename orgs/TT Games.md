# TT Games

## Overview

- **Full name:** TT Games Limited
- **Location:** Knutsford, England (headquarters)
- **Founded:** 2005 (merger of Traveller's Tales and Giant Interactive)
- **Parent:** Warner Bros. Games (since 2007, acquired for ~£100M)
- **Key people:** Michael Denny (studio head), Jonathan Smith (strategic manager)
- **Employees:** ~300 (estimated)
- **Union status:** No union
- **Website:** ttgames.com

## Subsidiaries

- **Traveller's Tales** — Main developer (founded 1989 by Jon Burton and Andy Ingram)
- **TT Games Publishing** — Publishing arm (formerly Giant Interactive)
- **TT Fusion** — Co-developer (formerly Embryonic Studios)
- **TT Odyssey** — Mobile game studio (formerly TT Games Brighton)
- TT Centroid — Motion capture studio

## History

Traveller's Tales was founded in 1989. The Lego video game franchise began with Lego Star Wars in 2005, leading to the merger of Traveller's Tales (developer) and Giant Interactive (publisher) to form TT Games.

**Warner acquisition (2007):** Warner Bros. paid ~£100M for 80% ownership (Jon Burton's share). TT Games became a key part of WB's children's/family gaming strategy.

**Expansion:** Acquired Embryonic Studios (TT Fusion) in 2007, Playdemic in 2017, and opened TT Odyssey in 2018.

**Skywalker Saga issues (2022):** Development was troubled. Reports revealed severe crunch culture, management conflicts, and the controversial NTT in-house engine which was extremely difficult to use. The game was the only TT title to use NTT; the company switched to Unreal Engine for future projects. Polygon and Fanbyte investigations detailed nepotism hires and dismissive management.

**Project cancellations (2023):** Multiple unannounced Lego projects cancelled, including a Lego Disney game that had been in development for at least 4 years. Other cancellations included "Project Cosmic" (Lego based on a major franchise). Since then, TT has not announced a new Lego game, breaking the near-annual release pattern they held for nearly two decades.

**Layoffs:** Multiple reports of layoffs at TT Games in 2023 and 2024 as part of WB's broader cost-cutting.

## Financial

- Historically TT Games was a reliable moneymaker for WB, pushing out annual Lego titles.
- The 2+ year gap since the last announcement suggests the studio is in a rebuilding/holding pattern.
- Lego gaming has moved toward Lego Fortnite (Epic Games) and other non-TT projects.

## Future

TT Games has been quiet since the 2023 cancellations. WB's refocus on core IPs (DC, Harry Potter) could mean new Lego games based on those properties, but the studio's future role within WB Games is uncertain.
