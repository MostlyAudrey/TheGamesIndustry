# Raven Software

**Call of Duty support studio founded in 1990 — the birthplace of the ABK union wave.** Based in Middleton, Wisconsin, Raven Software has primarily worked on Call of Duty since 2015 when it was folded into the COD rotation. The studio is best known for developing the Hexen series, Jedi Knight II, and more recently supporting Call of Duty: Warzone.

## Union history — the spark

Raven Software is the most significant studio in the ABK union story. In December 2021, Activision laid off ~12 of Raven's ~40 QA testers. The surviving QA workers walked out and began a five-week strike. At the end of the strike, they formed the Game Workers Alliance-CWA (GWA).

Key milestones:
- **Dec 2021:** 12 QA testers laid off; walkout begins
- **Jan 21, 2022:** GWA-CWA announced; Activision refuses voluntary recognition
- **Jan 25, 2022:** GWA files for NLRB election
- **Apr 22, 2022:** NLRB rules election can proceed
- **May 23, 2022:** GWA wins election 19-2 (86% support) — **first union at Activision Blizzard**
- **Aug 2025:** Raven QA ratifies first union contract — **first contract at any Activision Blizzard subsidiary**

The Raven union success directly led to:
1. The Microsoft-CWA Labor Neutrality Agreement (June 2022)
2. Blizzard Albany union (Dec 2022)
3. Activision QA United-CWA (Mar 2024)
4. The broader Blizzard wall-to-wall organizing wave (2024-2025)

The 2021 layoffs themselves were part of a pattern. Activision Blizzard was criticized for targeting QA workers — the department that had been organizing. The layoff and subsequent union drive became a national story and a pivotal moment in US labor history.
