# Monolith Soft

**Japanese video game developer and wholly owned Nintendo subsidiary.** Best known for the Xenoblade Chronicles series, one of Nintendo's flagship JRPG franchises. Founded in 1999 by Square alumni Tetsuya Takahashi and Hirohide Sugiura as a Namco subsidiary, acquired by Nintendo in 2007, and became 100% Nintendo-owned by December 2024.

## Key facts

- **344 employees** (as of 2025) across three studio locations in Tokyo and Kyoto
- **No union** as of October 2026
- **No layoffs** documented in the 2022-2026 period
- **Consistent headcount growth**: 120 (2014) → 243 (2020) → 299 (2024) → 344 (2025)
- **100% Nintendo-owned** since December 2024
- Notable for **promoting comfortable working environment with little to no overtime**

## Notable games

- Xenosaga series (2002-2006) — PS2 (under Namco)
- Xenoblade Chronicles (2010) — Wii
- Xenoblade Chronicles X (2015) — Wii U
- Xenoblade Chronicles 2 (2017) — Switch
- Xenoblade Chronicles 3 (2022) — Switch (+ DLC Future Redeemed 2023)
- Development support on The Legend of Zelda: Tears of the Kingdom (2023)
- Xenoblade Chronicles X: Definitive Edition (2025) — Switch

## Labor note

Monolith Soft stands out among Japanese game developers for explicitly promoting a healthy work environment with minimal overtime. The company's stated goals include allowing younger developers creative freedom and avoiding the crunch culture endemic to the broader Japanese game industry. As with all Nintendo subsidiaries in Japan, Monolith Soft employees are protected by Japanese labor law restrictions on layoffs and benefit from Nintendo's industry-low turnover culture.

## Key decisions

- 1999-10-01: Founded as Namco subsidiary by Tetsuya Takahashi, Hirohide Sugiura, Yasuyuki Honne
- 2007: Nintendo acquires Monolith Soft from Namco Bandai
- 2010: First Xenoblade game released for Wii to critical acclaim
- 2011: Kyoto studio established
- 2022: Xenoblade Chronicles 3 released
- 2023: Provides development support for Tears of the Kingdom
- 2024-12: Nintendo acquires remaining shares, becomes 100% owner
