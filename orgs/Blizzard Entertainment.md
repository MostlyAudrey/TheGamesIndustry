# Blizzard Entertainment

**Legendary American game developer and publisher, now the most unionized AAA studio in North America.** Founded 1991 as Silicon & Synapse, Blizzard created Warcraft, StarCraft, Diablo, Overwatch, and Hearthstone. Acquired by Activision in the 2008 Vivendi merger, then by Microsoft in 2023 as part of the $68.7B Activision Blizzard acquisition.

## Pre-Microsoft labor history

### ABK DFEH lawsuit and workplace crisis (2021)
The California DFEH lawsuit filed July 2021 specifically targeted Blizzard Entertainment's "frat boy" culture ([[sources/2021-07-21 — bloomberg-dfeh-activision-blizzard-lawsuit]]). Named Alex Afrasiabi, former World of Warcraft creative director, as central to allegations. Blizzard offices were reportedly well-stocked with alcohol ("cube crawls"), and the company had a history of treating top developers as "rock stars" immune from accountability.

The lawsuit triggered existential crisis at Blizzard:
- **July 28, 2021:** Thousands of employees walked out ([[sources/2021-07-28 — washpost-blizzard-employee-walkout]])
- **August 3, 2021:** Blizzard president J. Allen Brack departed; Jen Oneal and Mike Ybarra took over
- **November 2021:** Oneal left, citing being "tokenized, marginalized, and subject to gaslighting"
- The Wall Street Journal reported CEO Bobby Kotick was aware of misconduct and did little
- **December 2023:** DFEH lawsuit settled for $54 million; no admission of widespread harassment

### A Better ABK and walkouts
The employee activist group A Better ABK organized the July 2021 walkout and continued pressing for workplace reforms, including an end to forced arbitration, equitable pay, and hiring transparency. Their work laid the groundwork for the organizing surge that followed the Microsoft acquisition.

### Blizzard Albany QA union (2022)
The 20-member QA team at Blizzard Albany voted 14-0 to unionize as Game Workers Alliance Albany-CWA in July 2022 — the second union at an Activision Blizzard subsidiary ([[sources/2022-07-14 — polygon-blizzard-albany-qa-union]]).

## Post-acquisition: the organizing wave

### Microsoft Neutrality Agreement (2022)
Microsoft's Labor Neutrality Agreement with CWA was the critical enabler. Signed before the acquisition closed, it committed Microsoft to voluntary recognition and neutrality in union organizing. This agreement expired in Fall 2025 — a turning point in labor relations.

### Wall-to-wall organizing (2024-2025)
Under the protection of the neutrality agreement, Blizzard workers organized across game teams at unprecedented scale:

| Unit | Members | Organized |
|---|---|---|
| World of Warcraft (wall-to-wall) | ~500 | 2024 |
| Overwatch devs | ~200 | 2025 |
| Blizzard Story & Franchise | ~160 | 2025 |
| Diablo devs | ~450 | 2025 |
| Blizzard Platform & Technology | ~400+ | Oct 2025 |
| Blizzard Albany QA | ~20 | 2022 |

These six units formed the [[orgs/Blizzard CWA Unions (bargaining coalition)]] and bargained together as a coalition.

### Leadership change
After the January 2024 layoffs and Mike Ybarra's departure, Johanna Faries became Blizzard president on February 5, 2024 ([[sources/2024-01-29 — blizzard-entertainment-johanna-faries-announcement]]; [[sources/2024-01-29 — gameinformer-johanna-faries-blizzard-president]]). Faries came from the Call of Duty franchise — an unusual appointment that signaled Microsoft's emphasis on franchise management. She oversaw the entirety of the organizing wave and contract negotiations.

## The first contract — September 2026

**Largest single union contract victory in North American video game history.** Ratified in early September 2026, covering ~1,900 workers.

### Landmark terms ([[sources/2026-09-21 — labornotes-blizzard-first-contract]])
- **Recall rights:** 14 months; any open position across all Blizzard bargaining units
- **Severance:** Minimum 4 weeks + 1 week per 6 months tenure, up to 39 weeks
- **Just cause** and binding grievance/arbitration
- **Remote work guarantee:** Existing remote workers (~25% of covered workforce) protected
- **AI language:** Company must discuss, evaluate, and bargain over AI impact — one of first such provisions in major US tech union contract
- **Outsourcing protections:** Cannot lay off unless contractor hired in last year for similar job is let go; laid-off worker cannot be replaced by contractor for 1 year
- **Successorship:** If Microsoft sells unionized units, purchasing company must recognize the contract
- **Paid May 1** — International Workers' Day holiday
- **Hybrid:** 3-day in-office workweek

### Strategy: Pattern bargaining
The World of Warcraft unit set the contract template, which was then extended across all Blizzard units. This coalition bargaining approach helped standardize terms and maximize leverage.

## Current structure

Blizzard Entertainment operates as one of two operating units under the Activision Blizzard holding company, alongside [[orgs/Activision Publishing]]. It sits under [[orgs/Microsoft Gaming]] alongside Xbox Game Studios and ZeniMax Media/Bethesda.

- **President:** Johanna Faries (since Feb 2024)
- **Key studios:** Blizzard Albany (QA/support), Blizzard Boston (engineering)
- **Franchises:** Warcraft (WoW), Overwatch, Diablo, StarCraft, Hearthstone
- **Platform:** Battle.net online service

Blizzard retains its own publishing function — it publishes games developed by its internal studios, while Activision Publishing handles third-party relationships and Call of Duty.

### Post-contract landscape
- The Blizzard contract win created a stark contrast with [[orgs/Activision QA United-CWA]], which still lacks a first contract after 2.5 years
- Activision QA authorized a strike October 2026, highlighting Microsoft's different post-neutrality labor strategies across divisions
- The neutrality agreement's expiration (Fall 2025) has led to a tougher bargaining posture from Microsoft at Activision, while Blizzard organized early enough to secure terms while the agreement was still active
- Raven Software's earlier contract (August 2025) provided a template for Blizzard's pattern
