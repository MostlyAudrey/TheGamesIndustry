## Overview

Valve Corporation is a privately-held video game developer, publisher, and digital distribution company founded in 1996 by Gabe Newell and Mike Harrington (Harrington sold his stake in 2000). Headquartered in Bellevue, Washington. Valve is best known for Steam, the dominant PC gaming storefront, and for game franchises including Half-Life, Portal, Counter-Strike, Dota 2, and Left 4 Dead. The company is famously private, releases no public financial statements, and is owned entirely by founder Gabe Newell (majority, estimated >50%) with the remaining equity held via private grants to early employees and executives.

## Labor Model

Valve operates a famously flat organizational structure with no managers, no job titles, and a "self-directed" project allocation system. Key features:

- **No managers:** Since 2012, Valve has eliminated formal management. Employees choose which projects to work on.
- **Peer review compensation:** Annual, company-wide peer review system determines compensation adjustments. Stack ranking is used.
- **No job titles:** Employees have no formal titles or reporting structure.
- **High compensation:** Mean gross pay per employee estimated at ~$483K (2011) rising to ~$1.3M (2021), per leaked court documents [[sources/2024-07-17 — ars-valve-350-employees]]. Glassdoor reports average salary ~$107K (base $97K + $10K bonus) per Comparably data, but these likely undercount total compensation including Valve's profit-sharing/bonus system. Per leaked payroll data, total gross pay was ~$450M for ~350 employees in 2021.

Critiques from former employees include that the system can feel "like high school" (per Kotaku), that the salary review process is "nebulous and constantly changing" (per Glassdoor), and that the flat hierarchy can create informal power structures.

## Union Organizing

**No known union organizing efforts at Valve as of October 2026.** Valve has never had a union election, no public union campaign has been reported, and there is no organized labor presence. This places Valve as an outlier in the games industry, where unionization has spread to Activision, ZeniMax, Sega, Blizzard, and others during 2022-2026. Potential factors:
- Very small headcount (~350-384) and high compensation may reduce organizing interest.
- Flat hierarchy and "no bosses" structure may complicate traditional union framing.
- Washington State (where Valve is based) has weaker labor protections than California.

**Department breakdown (2021, from leaked Wolfire docs):**
- Games: Majority of headcount (Dota 2, CS2 support)
- Steam: 79 employees (down from 142 peak in 2015)
- Hardware: ~12% of headcount (Steam Deck, Valve Index)
- Admin: Small, but highest paid per capita

## Legal Cases & Controversies

### Active Litigation

1. **In re Valve Antitrust Litigation (Wolfire/Dark Catt)** — Class action certified Nov 2024, covering ~32,000 game developers alleging Steam's 30% commission and most-favored-nation pricing constitute illegal monopolization. Ninth Circuit declined Valve's appeal Jan 2025. Ongoing as of Oct 2026. [[events/2024-11-25 — Valve antitrust class action certified]] [[sources/2024-11-28 — gamesindustry-valve-class-action]]

2. **New York v. Valve Corp.** — Filed Feb 25, 2026 by NY AG Letitia James alleging loot boxes constitute illegal gambling. Valve has responded, comparing loot boxes to baseball cards. Ongoing. [[events/2026-02-25 — NY AG sues Valve over loot box gambling]] [[sources/2026-02-25 — reuters-ny-valve-lootbox]]

3. **UK class action (Vicki Shotbolt v. Valve)** — £656M ($838M-$904M) claim alleging Valve's pricing restrictions and Steam market dominance violate UK competition law. Valve lost attempt to throw out the claim in Jan 2026. [[sources/2026-01-27 — kotaku-valve-uk-class-action]]

### Past disputes

4. **Transgender discrimination lawsuit (2016-2018)** — Former Valve translator sued for $3.1M alleging transgender discrimination, hostile work environment, wrongful termination, and misclassification. Jury ruled in favor of Valve. [[sources/2018-01-10 — eurogamer-valve-transgender-verdict]]

5. **Mass arbitration campaign (2022-2024)** — Law firm organized mass arbitration against Valve; Valve responded by canceling forced arbitration Sept 2024 and accused the firm of "extortion." [[sources/2025-03-10 — reuters-valve-arbitration-cancel]]

### Dota 2 / Esports Controversies

- **TI prize pool collapse (2022-2024):** The International prize pool dropped from a peak of ~$40M (TI10, 2021) to ~$18.9M (TI11, 2022, no full battle pass), then to historically low levels in TI12 (2023) and TI13 (2024). Valve restructured away from the battle pass-funded model, stating that the massive prize pool was not healthy for the pro scene.
- **DPC cancellation (2022):** Valve canceled the Dota Pro Circuit, ending the league system and returning to a tournament-organizer based model.
- **Sponsor ban (2024/2025):** Valve banned in-game advertising of esports teams' sponsors, further affecting the Dota 2 esports ecosystem.
- **Skin economy crash (2025):** Valve's changes to CS2 skin trading reportedly wiped out over $1B in value.

### Moderation

Steam community moderation is largely outsourced to third-party contractors. Valve maintains a small internal Steam department (~79 employees as of 2021). The company uses a combination of paid third-party moderators, community volunteers, and developer representatives. There are no known layoff events specifically targeting moderation staff, as moderation has been consistently outsourced.

## Key Takeaways (Labor Perspective)

- **No layoffs documented (2022-2026)** — Valve is genuinely an outlier in the games industry for having no known layoff events. However, the small headcount makes layoffs less likely to be newsworthy.
- **No union activity** — One of the few major US game companies with no union effort.
- **High compensation** — Likely a union-dampening factor. Mean total compensation per employee is among highest in tech.
- **Flat hierarchy has trade-offs** — Former employees report informal power structures, nebulous review processes, and a "high school" social dynamic.
- **Major legal exposure** — The antitrust class action represents significant legal risk, though it targets Steam's business model rather than labor practices.
- **No PE/VC involvement** — Valve's independence from outside capital makes it structurally different from most major game companies tracked in this project.
