# HoYoverse (miHoYo)

**Chinese video game developer and publisher, HQ Shanghai.** Best known for Genshin Impact, Honkai series, and Zenless Zone Zero. Private company (founder-owned), no public listing. Global publishing brand HoYoverse (Cognosphere Pte. Ltd.) based in Singapore since 2022.

**Current status (Oct 2026):** Still growing but at a much slower pace. After explosive growth from 2020-2024, hiring has cooled significantly (86 new postings/month in 2023 → 15/month in 2026). Headcount appears to have slightly contracted in 2025 before recovering modestly in 2026. The company remains privately held with massive cash reserves (estimated $7B+ in assets). New office campus in Shanghai planned for 6,000-8,000 workers suggests long-term growth ambitions.

## Labor practices — Genshin Impact crunch

miHoYo/HoYoverse presents a **split reputation** on labor:

**The "best employer" narrative:**
- Reported as **#1 best Chinese company to work for** in a 2024 survey (as of 2024-08, [[sources/2024-08-03 — reddit-zzz-hoyoverse-best-employer]])
- Ranked #48 amongst all private businesses in China (2025)
- High compensation by Chinese standards
- Strong brand cachet in tech/gaming sector
- Expansive Shanghai HQ with premium amenities
- Plans to build massive new campus for 6,000-8,000 workers (as of 2023, [[sources/2026-10-18 — wiki-mihoyo-structure]])

**The crunch narrative:**
- Glassdoor reviews describe "lack of work-life balance," overtime "expected and normalized," and "996 culture" ([[sources/2026-10-18 — glassdoor-hoyoverse-culture-reviews]])
- Genshin Impact's relentless content schedule (major update every 6 weeks) necessitates sustained overtime
- Multiple Reddit threads from employees and industry observers describe studio as high-pressure with unpaid overtime endemic
- "996" work schedule (9am-9pm, 6 days/week) is common in Chinese tech — though miHoYo has not been specifically confirmed to enforce it, Glassdoor reviews allege the culture is comparable
- One former employee described: "a sword hanging on their heads, which can go down anytime" (as of 2021, Reddit)

**Contradiction explanation:** China's tech labor market compensates highly for extreme hours. "Best employer" rankings often weight compensation, brand prestige, and facilities over work-life balance. miHoYo can simultaneously be a high-pressure crunch environment and a top-ranked employer in the Chinese context.

## Layoffs

**No major confirmed layoffs** at miHoYo China. The company continued hiring through the 2022-2024 industry-wide downturn, bucking the trend. However:

- **2025:** Unconfirmed reports of layoffs at HoYoverse Western branches, primarily affecting recruitment staff and Genshin Impact writers ([[sources/2025-01-10 — reddit-hoyoverse-unannounced-layoffs]])
- Singapore operations reportedly untouched; miHoYo China unaffected
- Hiring velocity dropped sharply — from 86/month (2023) to 15/month (2026) — indicating a quiet hiring freeze/slowdown
- Headcount dipped from ~6,677 (end 2024) to ~5,245 (2025), a decline of ~1,400, before recovering to ~5,469 (2026)

The headcount dip and hiring slowdown may reflect:
1. Natural maturation after rapid expansion (2020-2024)
2. Efficiency improvements as live-ops teams stabilized
3. Quiet trimming of underperforming hires (common in Chinese tech)
4. Possible undisclosed layoffs consistent with Reddit reports

## Union organizing

**No known union organizing** at miHoYo/HoYoverse — in China, Japan, Singapore, or Western offices. Several factors:

- **China:** Independent unions illegal; enterprise-level unions are state-controlled ACFTU affiliates. miHoYo would have a mandatory enterprise union but it's not an independent worker organization.
- **Singapore:** Labor law restricts union formation (only NTUC-affiliated unions permitted, employer recognition required).
- **Western offices (Montreal, LA):** These small outposts could theoretically organize under CWA Canada or UVW-CWA, but no known campaigns exist.
- **Cultural factors:** High compensation, prestige, and competitive hiring pool reduce organizing incentive.

## Key observations

1. **No major layoffs unlike NetEase**, but hiring slowdown consistent with industry contraction
2. **Crunch well-documented** but normalized within Chinese tech context; company paradoxically also "best employer"
3. **No union activity** at any location — structural barriers in China/Singapore + cultural disincentives
4. **Privately owned** — no shareholder pressure, which may insulate from certain layoff pressures
5. **Massive financial reserves** ($7B+ assets) reduce layoff incentive compared to public competitors
6. **Growth deceleration** from 2023 peak to 2026 suggests maturation rather than distress
