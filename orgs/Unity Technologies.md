# Unity Technologies — Labor and Corporate Profile

## Overview

Unity Technologies (NYSE: U) is a San Francisco-based video game engine and development platform company. Founded in 2004, Unity grew to dominate the indie/mobile game engine market, powering over 50% of mobile games globally. The company went public in September 2020.

Between 2022 and 2025, Unity underwent **six rounds of layoffs** cutting an estimated **~3,200+ jobs**, triggered by a combination of post-IPO overexpansion, the disastrous Runtime Fee pricing change, CEO turnover, and aggressive cost-cutting under investor pressure.

## Layoff timeline

### 2022 — First cuts
- **June 2022:** ~225 jobs eliminated (first reported wave). Unity framed as a reorganization.

### 2023 — Three rounds in one year
- **January 2023:** ~284 jobs cut (second wave). Coming six months after the June 2022 round.
- **May 2023:** 600 jobs (8% of workforce). Second major round. Unity said this was for "long-term and profitable growth."
- **November 2023:** 265 jobs (3.8% of workforce). Majority (256 of 265) from the Weta Digital division, after Unity terminated its Weta FX agreement. Part of the "company reset" announced after the Runtime Fee crisis.

### 2024 — The big one
- **January 2024:** 1,800 jobs (25% of workforce). **Unity's largest layoff ever.** Announced via SEC filing on Jan 8. All teams affected. The layoffs were described as a continuation of the "company reset." Stock rose ~5% on the announcement. Estimated cost: $205M. Completed by end of Q1 2024. James Whitehurst (interim CEO since Oct 2023) presided over this round.

### 2025 — More cuts under Bromberg
- **February 2025:** Undisclosed number of jobs eliminated. Sixth wave since 2022. Entire Behavior department (NPC scripting tools) eliminated. Employees notified via 5AM email. CEO Bromberg acknowledged "exhaustion associated with prior changes." The memo signaled a strategic pivot toward "fidelity for ubiquity," live services, generative AI, and platform extensibility.

## CEO Cycle

| CEO | Tenure | Context |
|---|---|---|
| **John Riccitiello** | 2014 — Oct 2023 | Led Unity through IPO (2020) and massive expansion. Resigned after Runtime Fee debacle. Former EA CEO. |
| **James M. Whitehurst** | Oct 2023 — May 2024 | Interim CEO. Former Red Hat CEO. Presided over the 1,800 layoff round. Stepped down when permanent replacement found. |
| **Matthew Bromberg** | May 15, 2024 — present | Former Zynga COO and EA SVP. Canceled Runtime Fee. Continued layoffs. Shifted strategy toward adtech, live services, and cross-platform. |

Chairman: Roelof Botha (Sequoia Capital partner, replaced Riccitiello as chairman Oct 2023).

## Runtime Fee Crisis

- **Sep 12, 2023:** Unity announced per-install "Runtime Fee" for developers above revenue/install thresholds
- **Sep 2023:** Massive developer backlash; boycott of Unity Ads; indie developers threatened engine migration
- **Sep 22, 2023:** Unity partially revised the fee structure
- **Oct 9, 2023:** CEO John Riccitiello resigned
- **Sep 12, 2024:** New CEO Matthew Bromberg fully canceled the Runtime Fee, replacing it with subscription price increases for larger customers

The Runtime Fee was Unity's most damaging business decision, triggering a leadership crisis, massive layoffs, and long-term developer trust erosion.

## Union organizing at Unity

**No certified union at Unity as of October 2026.** Unlike Microsoft/Activision Blizzard (CWA-organized), Epic Games (Bandcamp United — divested), or Ubisoft (STJV/CWA Canada), Unity has no public union organizing campaign or certified bargaining unit.

However, Unity workers would be eligible to join **United Videogame Workers-CWA Local 9433 (UVW-CWA)**, the industry-wide direct-join union launched in March 2025 that is open to all video game workers in the US and Canada regardless of employer or employment status. UVW-CWA reported approaching 600 members by early 2026.

The broader industry organizing context (CODE-CWA having helped 6,500+ tech/game workers organize since 2020) makes Unity a potential target for future organizing, particularly given:
- The scale of job destruction (3,200+ layoffs from a peak of ~7,200)
- The impersonal execution tactics (5AM notification emails cited in Feb 2025 round)
- Multiple CEO changes in 18 months suggesting governance instability
- The UVW-CWA model removing employer-based barriers to joining

## Current headcount and state

Headcount data varies by source:

| Source | Year | Count | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Macrotrends (SEC filings) | FY 2023 | ~6,745 | Pre-layoff peak approximate |
| Macrotrends (SEC filings) | FY 2024 | 4,987 | Post-1,800 layoff; 26.1% decline from 2023 |
| Macrotrends (SEC filings) | FY 2025 | 4,412 | 11.53% decline from 2024 |
| Revelio Labs | 2026 | ~6,708 | Likely includes ironSource acquisition headcount |

The discrepancy between Macrotrends (~4,400 for FY 2025) and Revelio Labs (~6,700 for 2026) likely reflects different consolidation scopes — Revelio may count the full ironSource entity. The actual core Unity headcount appears to have stabilized around 4,400-5,000 after the layoff cycle.

Unity's stock (NYSE: U) has fallen from its November 2021 peak of ~$160 to the $15-30 range in 2025-2026, reflecting continued investor skepticism about the company's turnaround.
