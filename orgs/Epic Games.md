# Epic Games

> US game publisher, Unreal Engine developer, and Fortnite creator. Headquarters: Cary, North Carolina. Privately held, controlled by CEO Tim Sweeney. Post-money valuation of $31.5B as of April 2022 ([[sources/2023-09-28 — variety-epic-830-layoffs-bandcamp]]).

## Key facts

- **Founded:** 1991 by Tim Sweeney (as Potomac Computer Systems, renamed Epic MegaGames, then Epic Games)
- **Ownership:** Private; controlled by CEO Tim Sweeney. Sony and KIRKBI (LEGO owner) each invested $1B at $31.5B valuation in 2022 ([[sources/2023-09-28 — variety-epic-830-layoffs-bandcamp]]).
- **Flagship products:** Fortnite, Unreal Engine, Epic Games Store, Fall Guys (via Mediatonic), Rocket League (via Psyonix)

## Labor events

### September 2023 — 830 layoffs (16% of workforce)

**Announced Sep 28, 2023.** Largest single-day layoff in company history.

- **830 employees laid off** (16% of workforce). Additionally, ~250 leaving through the Bandcamp and SuperAwesome divestitures ([[sources/2023-09-28 — variety-epic-830-layoffs-bandcamp]]).
- **Reason:** CEO Tim Sweeney said Epic was "spending way more money than we earn." Fortnite's creator ecosystem had shifted to lower-margin revenue sharing. Prior cost-cutting (net zero hiring, reduced marketing/events) was insufficient ([[sources/2023-09-28 — variety-epic-830-layoffs-bandcamp]]).
- **Affected teams:** Sweeney said "two-thirds of the layoffs were in teams outside of core development" ([[sources/2023-09-29 — eurogamer-mediatonic-layoffs]]). Mediatonic (Fall Guys) was hit particularly hard across IT support, marketing, software development, character art, gameplay programming, level design, production, and narrative roles; Bloomberg reported the studio was "hit very hard" but remained open ([[sources/2023-09-29 — eurogamer-mediatonic-layoffs]]).
- **Divestitures alongside layoffs:**
  - **Bandcamp** sold to Songtradr (music licensing company). Bandcamp had 118 employees; 60 received offers from Songtradr, 58 did not ([[sources/2023-10-17 — gamesindustry-bandcamp-50pct-layoffs]]). Dismissed staff received 6 months severance; Bandcamp United union negotiated for better terms ([[sources/2023-10-17 — gamesindustry-bandcamp-50pct-layoffs]]).
  - **SuperAwesome** ad business spun off as independent company under CEO Kate O'Loughlin; Kids Web Services (KWS) remained at Epic ([[sources/2023-09-28 — variety-epic-830-layoffs-bandcamp]]).
- **Legal context:** Epic noted it was reducing legal expenses but continuing antitrust fights against Apple and Google ([[sources/2023-09-28 — variety-epic-830-layoffs-bandcamp]]).

### March 2026 — 1,000+ layoffs (second major wave)

**Announced Mar 24, 2026.** Second major layoff round in three years.

- **1,000+ employees laid off** (~20% of workforce) ([[sources/2026-03-24 — bbc-epic-1000-layoffs]]).
- **Reason:** CEO Tim Sweeney attributed to a "downturn" in Fortnite engagement beginning in 2025, leaving Epic "spending significantly more than we're making." Also cited industry-wide slowed growth, weakened spending, and increased competition ([[sources/2026-03-24 — bbc-epic-1000-layoffs]]).
- Sweeney announced $500M in cost savings across other operations alongside the layoffs ([[sources/2026-03-24 — bbc-epic-1000-layoffs]]).
- Sweeney stated specifically: "the layoffs aren't related to AI" ([[sources/2026-03-24 — bbc-epic-1000-layoffs]]).
- Sweeney acknowledged unique struggles: delivering "consistent Fortnite magic with every season" and a full mobile experience after Apple/Google legal battles ([[sources/2026-03-24 — bbc-epic-1000-layoffs]]).
- Sweeney apologized: "I'm sorry we're here again" ([[sources/2026-03-24 — bbc-epic-1000-layoffs]]).

### October 2026 — AI pivot

- CEO Tim Sweeney announced a "huge pivot" toward AI game development on Oct 12, 2026, months after the 1,000+ layoffs ([[sources/2026-10-12 — dotesports-epic-ai-pivot]]). Note: the March layoff memo had specifically denied AI was a factor in the cuts.

## Union and labor activity

### Bandcamp United — first union at Epic Games

- **Bandcamp United** announced formation March 16, 2023 ([[sources/2023-03-16 — techcrunch-bandcamp-united-union]]). Includes engineers, writers, project managers, support staff, and designers at Bandcamp.
- **Won union election 31-7** on May 19, 2023, making it the first union under Epic Games ([[sources/2023-05-22 — gamedeveloper-bandcamp-union-victory]]).
- **Represented by** Office and Professional Employees International Union (OPEIU) ([[sources/2023-05-22 — gamedeveloper-bandcamp-union-victory]]).
- **Union busting allegations:** Epic and Bandcamp CEO Ethan Diamond were accused of union busting tactics (delaying NLRB process) before the vote. Diamond refuted claims, stating he was "following the NLRB's process and timeline" ([[sources/2023-04-06 — gamedeveloper-bandcamp-union-busting]]).
- **Post-vote:** Joint statement committing to good-faith bargaining ([[sources/2023-05-22 — gamedeveloper-bandcamp-union-victory]]).

### NLRB labor complaint (2022)

- An NLRB complaint was filed against Epic Games in April 2022, alleging the company refused to hire a former Activision Blizzard and Apple employee because of her labor advocacy work with the Apple Together movement ([[sources/2022-04-29 — gamesindustry-epic-nlrb-complaint]]).
- After four rounds of interviews, she disclosed her labor advocacy on Epic's Request for Outside Activity form; Epic told her the role went to another candidate two days later ([[sources/2022-04-29 — gamesindustry-epic-nlrb-complaint]]).
- Epic denied the claim, saying the candidate "scored higher in their interviews" and the form "did not play any role in our decision" ([[sources/2022-04-29 — gamesindustry-epic-nlrb-complaint]]).
- NLRB investigated; outcome not confirmed in available sources.

## Ongoing legal context

- Epic Games has been in multiyear antitrust litigation against **Apple** (filed 2020; appeals court ruled for Apple April 2023) and **Google** (trial win Dec 2023; ongoing remedies).
- Reduced legal expenses cited as part of 2023 restructuring, but Epic stated it would "continue the fight against Apple and Google distribution monopolies" ([[sources/2023-09-28 — variety-epic-830-layoffs-bandcamp]]).

## Key personnel

- **Tim Sweeney** — Founder and CEO. Controls the company. Public-facing on all major decisions (layoff memos, AI pivot, legal strategy).
- **Elka Looks** — Spokesperson (as of 2022 NLRB complaint).
