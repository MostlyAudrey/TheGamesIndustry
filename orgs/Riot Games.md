# Riot Games

**US games publisher/developer, HQ Los Angeles, CA. Founded 2006 by Brandon Beck and Marc Merrill. Fully owned by Tencent since 2015.**

Developer and publisher of **League of Legends** (flagship, launched 2009), **Valorant** (2020), **Teamfight Tactics**, **Legends of Runeterra**, **Wild Rift**, and **2XKO** (2026). Operates the **League of Legends Championship Series (LCS)** esports league.

## Workplace Culture

Riot's workplace culture drew national scrutiny beginning with a **Kotaku exposé in 2018** that described a "bro culture" with systemic gender discrimination and harassment. This led to:

1. **Class action lawsuit** (2018) — settled for $100M in 2021 (final approval 2023), the largest gender discrimination settlement in California civil rights history.
2. **Employee walkout** (May 6, 2019) — first known walkout in the video game industry. 150-200 employees protested forced arbitration.
3. **Workplace reforms** — 3-year independent monitoring (now concluded), pay transparency, revised hiring/promotion practices.

## Unionization

**No formal union** has been certified at Riot Games itself as of late 2026. Key labor organizing context:

- **LCS Players Association (2017)** — Riot created and funded a professional organization for esports players in the League of Legends Championship Series. Legal scholars questioned whether this violated the NLRA (employer funding of a labor organization). Riot argued it was a professional organization, not a union, since players could not collectively bargain or strike.
- **Industry-wide** — CWA's Campaign to Organize Digital Employees (CODE-CWA, launched 2020) and Game Workers Unite have organized at other studios (Activision Blizzard QA unions, etc.). UVW-CWA Local 9433 launched at GDC 2025. But Riot has not been a target of a successful certification campaign.

## Tencent ownership context

Riot has been fully owned by Chinese conglomerate Tencent since 2015. Tencent is the world's largest video game company by revenue. The layoff decisions appear to have been made independently by Riot's leadership (CEO Dylan Jadeja), but Tencent's ownership provides the financial backstop.
