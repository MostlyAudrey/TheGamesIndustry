# Nintendo

**Japan's largest video game company; one of the 'Big Three' platform holders** alongside Sony and Microsoft. HQ Kyoto, Japan. Listed on Tokyo Stock Exchange (TYO: 7974). CEO Shuntaro Furukawa (since 2018). US operations based in Redmond, WA under Nintendo of America (NOA, president Doug Bowser until ~2026).

**Distinct labor profile:** Nintendo is the only major global publisher to have *zero mass layoffs of permanent employees* in the 2022-2026 period, enabled by Japanese labor law protections (which make layoffs nearly impossible absent severe financial distress) and consistent profitability. However, its US subsidiary has faced persistent labor complaints.

## Key findings

**Japan (Nintendo Co., Ltd.):** No documented layoffs of permanent employees in the 2022-2026 period. The company has grown its workforce and increased base wages. Retention rate 98.1% in Japan; average tenure 14.4 years. No union organizing activity documented in Japan. Japanese labor law effectively prohibits layoffs of regular employees unless the company faces existential financial distress.

**Nintendo of America (Redmond, WA):** The primary site of labor conflict:

1. **Union-busting allegations (2022):** The NLRB complaint established a pattern of hostility toward organizing. The $25,910 settlement required Nintendo to post a notice informing workers of their right to unionize — a standard NLRB settlement term.

2. **Toxic workplace culture (2022):** The Kotaku investigation found a structural two-tier system where contractors ("red badges") were paid less, had fewer protections, and feared retaliation for reporting harassment by full-time employees. Power imbalance between full-time staff and contract workers was a central theme.

3. **Contractor layoffs (2024):** ~100+ contractor positions cut in Product Testing. Nintendo characterized this as a reorganization to create full-time roles. However, the layoffs hit at a time when the broader industry was already shedding jobs by the thousands.

4. **New NLRB complaints (2025-2026):** Fresh charges signal continued organizing pressure at NOA. The involvement of a different contracting firm (Teksystems, vs. Aston Carter in 2022) suggests the labor issues are structural, not tied to a single vendor.

**Context:** Nintendo's labor experience is the mirror opposite of Sony and Microsoft. While both platform holders conducted mass layoffs (Sony: ~900 in Feb 2024; Microsoft: 6,100+ between Jan 2024 and Jul 2026), Nintendo made zero permanent staff cuts. However, reliance on contract workers creates a parallel workforce without the same protections.
