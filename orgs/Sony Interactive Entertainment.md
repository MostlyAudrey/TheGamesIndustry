# Sony Interactive Entertainment (PlayStation)

**Sony Interactive Entertainment (SIE)** is the video game division of Sony Group Corporation, operating the PlayStation platform and a network of first-party game development studios (PlayStation Studios). HQ in San Mateo, CA with corporate parent in Tokyo.

## Labor timeline (2022-2026)

### 2021 — Japan Studio effectively closed
Sony restructured Japan Studio around Team ASOBI (Astro Bot). Legendary studio behind Ape Escape, Shadow of the Colossus, Ico effectively dissolved. Sony stated it no longer wanted AA games.

### 2023 — PixelOpus closure
Small studio behind Concrete Genie closed (June 2023). Internal review deemed it didn't meet strategic objectives.

### 2024-02-27 — The 900 layoffs
**Largest labor event in SIE history.** Announced by outgoing CEO Jim Ryan:
- **~900 jobs** (~8% of SIE workforce)
- **London Studio closed entirely** after 20 years
- **Firesprite** (Liverpool) — major reductions (never recovered)
- **Naughty Dog** — ~25 contract devs laid off
- **Insomniac Games, Guerrilla Games** — reductions
- Affected all regions: Americas, EMEA, Japan, APAC
- London officially shut its doors May 2024
- Context: PS5 sales target miss ($10B stock drop), near-decade low gaming margins
- Jim Ryan retired March 2024; Hiroki Totoki interim; co-CEOs Hulst/Nishino established June 2024

### 2024-10-29 — Firewalk + Neon Koi closures
- **Firewalk Studios** (Bellevue, WA): ~170 jobs — Concord cost $200-400M, launched Aug 2024, pulled weeks later
- **Neon Koi** (ex-Savage Game Studios): ~40 jobs — mobile studio, never released a game
- CWA issued call for unionization at SIE

### 2025-06-10 — Bend Studio layoffs
~40 jobs (~30% of staff) at Days Gone developer. Live-service project canceled. Part of SIE's retreat from live-service games after Concord.

### 2026-02-19 — Bluepoint Games closure
~70 jobs. Acclaimed remake studio (Shadow of the Colossus, Demon's Souls). Live-service God of War canceled Jan 2025. Third SIE studio closure in two years.

### 2026-03-25 — Dark Outlaw Games closure
Led by former Treyarch devs/staffed by Deviation Games remnants. Never announced a game.

## Union presence
SIE has **no recognized unions** at any first-party studio as of Oct 2026. However:

- **CWA** filed an unfair labor practice charge against Sony for restricting workers' right to distribute leaflets (concerted activity)
- **CWA statement** (Oct 31, 2024) explicitly called on SIE workers to organize after Firewalk/Neon Koi closures
- **UVW-CWA Local 9433** (United Video Game Workers) launched March 2025 as an industry-wide union; ~600 members by early 2026; open to SIE workers but no specific SIE bargaining unit confirmed
- Former Sony Computer Entertainment Europe president Chris Deering controversially suggested laid-off workers "drive an Uber or go to the beach for a year" (mid-2024)
- CWA has organized over 2,000 video game workers and 5,000 tech workers in the US

## Net labor flow assessment
Six distinct layoff events documented from Feb 2024 through Mar 2026:
| Event | Date | Jobs |
|---|---|---|
| SIE global restructure | Feb 2024 | ~900 |
| Firewalk closure | Oct 2024 | ~170 |
| Neon Koi closure | Oct 2024 | ~40 |
| Bend Studio cuts | Jun 2025 | ~40 |
| Bluepoint closure | Feb 2026 | ~70 |
| Dark Outlaw closure | Mar 2026 | undisclosed |
| **Total confirmed** | | **~1,220+** |

Plus two earlier closures (PixelOpus 2023, Japan Studio 2021) with undisclosed counts. Official SIE G&NS headcount fell from 12,700 (Mar 2024) to 12,100 (Mar 2025) — a net decline of ~600.

**Overall: Eight first-party studios closed since PS5 launch. 60% of Sony's post-2019 studio acquisitions saw layoffs or shutdowns. No unionized studios. Net labor flow strongly negative since 2024.**

## Context: the live-service pivot and its aftermath
- 2022: Sony acquired Bungie ($3.6B) for live-service expertise; announced 10 live-service games target
- 2024: Concord disaster ($200-400M loss) killed the strategy
- 2025-2026: mass cancellations of live-service projects, leading to studio closures and layoffs
- At least 1,250+ confirmed job losses, likely exceeding 1,400 including undisclosed events
