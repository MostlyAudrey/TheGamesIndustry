# Capcom

**Major Japanese video game publisher and developer.** HQ Osaka, Japan. Listed on Tokyo Stock Exchange (TYO: 9697). Known for Resident Evil, Monster Hunter, Street Fighter, Devil May Cry, and Mega Man. CEO Haruhiro Tsujimoto.

**Distinct labor profile:** Capcom is notable for having *zero layoffs of permanent employees* during the 2022-2026 global gaming industry crisis. Instead, the company raised salaries — a pattern shared by several Japanese publishers and enabled by Japanese labor law protections. Union organizing inside Capcom is effectively absent; the only union-related dispute is a SAG-AFTRA performer boycott of a Capcom-licensed title.

## Key findings

**No layoffs (Japan):** Capcom Japan has had zero documented layoffs of permanent employees in the 2022-2026 period. Like Nintendo, this is enabled by Japanese employment law and the company's consistent profitability. Capcom Vancouver was closed in 2018 (pre-period).

**Salary increases as alternative to layoffs:** Capcom has invested in wages during a period when Western publishers were cutting thousands of jobs. The 28% starting salary increase for new graduates was explicitly framed as talent retention and employee investment. Capcom's HR reforms (Chief HR Officer, bonus system, stock compensation) suggest a systematic effort to improve labor conditions — or to forestall unionization pressure seen in Western game companies.

**No internal union:** No union exists at Capcom Japan or Capcom USA. The 2021 controversy over union statements (Business Journal alleging Capcom does not allow unions; Capcom saying employees are free to form them) appears to have been the full extent of union discussion — no organizing efforts have been publicly reported since.

**SAG-AFTRA dispute (2026):** Capcom's refusal to sign a union contract for Mega Man: Dual Override is the company's most significant union-related event in the 2022-2026 period. This is a performer union dispute (voice actors), not an internal employee organizing effort, but indicates Capcom's resistance to union terms even when working with US-based talent and licensors.

**Vancouver closure (2018, pre-period):** Capcom Vancouver was shut down in September 2018 after ~50 staff were laid off earlier that year (~30% of the studio). The closure affected ~150+ staff total. The studio was the developer behind the Dead Rising series. This event predates the 2022-2026 focus period but is Capcom's most significant labor reduction event in recent memory.

## Scope note
Capcom Co., Ltd. is a Japanese publisher outside the US/Canada primary scope of Project Gamecraft.
