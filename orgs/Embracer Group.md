# Embracer Group AB

**Embracer Group AB** (formerly THQ Nordic AB, formerly Nordic Games Licensing AB) is a Swedish video game and media holding company headquartered in Karlstad, Sweden. Founded by Lars Wingefors, it grew through an aggressive acquisition spree (2019-2022) to become Europe's largest video game holding company by studio count, then underwent a dramatic restructuring (2023-2024) after a $2 billion investment deal collapsed.

## The Failed $2B Saudi Investment

In June 2022, **Savvy Games Group** — wholly owned by Saudi Arabia's **Public Investment Fund (PIF)** — invested approximately $1 billion in Embracer for an 8.1% stake. Simultaneously, a larger $2 billion investment deal had been verbally agreed in October 2022 for additional funding toward new game development. In May 2023, Savvy Games Group walked away after protracted negotiations. Matthew Karch (Saber Interactive CEO) said the deal grew too large as a "single monolithic approach" for Savvy, expanding from initial Turok/Jurassic Park funding to covering development across the entire group. Embracer's shares fell 40% on the announcement. [[sources/2023-05-24 — axios-embracer-deal-collapse]] [[sources/2023-08-14 — axios-savvy-embracer]]

The deal's collapse left Embracer with over $2 billion in debt from its acquisition spree, triggering the restructuring.

## Restructuring Program (June 2023 – May 2024)

Announced June 2023 and declared "successfully finalised" by CEO Wingefors in May 2024, the restructuring encompassed:

**Scale of destruction (June 2023 – May 2024):**
- **4,532 employees** lost (approximately 29% of peak workforce)
- **44 studios closed**
- **80 in-development projects canceled** (including new Deus Ex, TimeSplitters, and Red Faction games)
- Debt reduced from $2B to $1.5B (still in place)

**Workforce trajectory:**
- Peak: ~15,701 employees (early 2023)
- After Saber sale: ~10,450 (Nov 2024)
- End of 2024: 7,873
- End of March 2026: 6,090
- August 2026: ~5,000 (across both Embracer and Fellowship Entertainment segments)

## Corporate Split (2024–present)

In April 2024, Embracer announced a plan to transform into four standalone publicly listed entities:

1. **Embracer** (remaining entity, rebranding from Embracer Group) — THQ Nordic (35 studios), Plaion, Deca Games, Freemode, Tripwire Interactive, Aspyr, Beamdog, Limited Run Games, Vertigo Games
2. **Fellowship Entertainment** (to spin off 2027) — Crystal Dynamics, Eidos-Montréal, Warhorse Studios, 4A Games, Dark Horse Media, Flying Wild Hog; IPs: Tomb Raider, Lord of the Rings, Deus Ex, Metro, Dead Island, Kingdom Come: Deliverance
3. **Coffee Stain Group** (spun off Dec 2025) — Coffee Stain Studios, Ghost Ship Games, Tuxedo Labs, Amplifier Game Invest Scandinavian studios
4. **Asmodee Group** (spun off Feb 2025) — Board games publishing

CEO transition: **Lars Wingefors** resigned as CEO in June 2025, replaced by **Phil Rogers** (formerly Eidos/Square Enix, CEO of Plaion and Crystal Dynamics-Eidos) effective August 2025. Wingefors became chairman. [[sources/2025-06-27 — vgc-embracer-ceo-replacement]]

## Union Organizing in European Studios

Limited information is available about formal union organizing at Embracer's European studios. As a Swedish company operating under Swedish and German labor law, most Embracer studios operate with statutory **works councils** (Betriebsrat in Germany) and collective bargaining agreements, rather than US-style union certification drives. Key context:

- **Sweden**: The Swedish model relies on collective bargaining agreements (kollektivavtal) between employer organizations and unions (Unionen, Sveriges Ingenjörer, Akademikerförbundet SSR). Embracer's Swedish studios (THQ Nordic, Tarsier, etc.) are covered by these sectoral agreements. The restructuring triggered consultations with these unions, but specific organizing drives have not been widely reported in English-language press.

- **Germany**: German studios under Embracer (Piranha Bytes, Pow Wow Entertainment, etc.) had statutory works councils, but these did not prevent closures. IG Metall is the relevant union for German game developers.

- **UK**: Free Radical Design (Nottingham) was closed without reported union organizing effort. UK game workers are covered by Unite the Union / IWGB but no known bargaining unit existed.

- **France**: No major Embracer French studio closures reported; French labor law requires economic redundancy consultations.

- **Overall**: No known US-style union certification campaigns (CWA/UVW) at Embracer's European studios. The European labor framework (works councils, sectoral bargaining) provided procedural consultation rights but did not prevent the mass layoffs or closures.

*Note: English-language reporting on European union organizing at Embracer is sparse. Further research in Swedish (Unionen, Sveriges Ingenjörer) and German (IG Metall) sources is needed for complete labor coverage.*

## Current State (October 2026)

- **Two operating segments**: Embracer (~2,869 employees, 30 studios) and Fellowship Entertainment (~2,071 employees, 9 studios) across 30 countries
- **Total workforce**: ~5,000 (down from peak of ~15,701)
- **Remaining debt**: ~$1.5B (reduced from $2B)
- **Stock**: Traded on Nasdaq Stockholm (EMBRAC B)
- **Leadership**: Phil Rogers (CEO), Lars Wingefors (chairman)
- **Future**: Fellowship Entertainment spin-off targeted for 2027; Embracer Group to rebrand as Embracer
- **Strategy**: "Simply making better products and games" — no plans for further M&A
- **Ongoing divestitures**: Continued non-core asset sales through 2025-2026
