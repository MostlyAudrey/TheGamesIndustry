# NetEase, Inc.

**Chinese internet and gaming conglomerate, HQ Hangzhou.** Publicly traded on Nasdaq (NTES) and HKEX (9999). Gaming is the primary revenue driver, generating the vast majority of the company's CN¥112.6B (2025) revenue.

**Current status (Oct 2026):** Undergoing significant strategic restructuring under founder/CEO William Ding, who shifted from hands-off approach to direct involvement in game division. NetEase has pulled back from international investments, closed or divested multiple Western studios, and prioritized live-service "evergreen" titles. Despite layoffs, the company reported $11.2B revenue for FY2024-2025.

## Union organizing

**No known union organizing at any NetEase studio** — neither in China (where independent unions are state-controlled) nor at its Western subsidiaries (US, Canada, UK). This contrasts with many other major game publishers where CWA-affiliated unions have formed. Chinese labor law requires enterprise-level trade unions but these are not independent worker organizations.

## Development structure and headcount

NetEase Games is the company's primary game development and publishing division. The company operates a mix of:
- **Internal China-based studios** (Guangzhou, Hangzhou, Beijing, Shanghai) — core development for major franchises
- **Overseas subsidiaries** — mostly newer (post-2020) Western studios set up for global expansion
- **Acquired studios** — Skybox Labs (Vancouver), Ouka Studios (Japan)

## Key observations

1. **No union activity** detected at NetEase, unlike many Western peers. NetEase's Western studios (US, Canada, UK) could theoretically organize under CWA or UVW-CWA, but no known campaigns exist.
2. **Layoff pattern**: NetEase's cuts track with CEO Ding's profit-first pivot. Western studios were hit hardest while China teams were redirected. The company denied "wide scale layoffs" despite Bloomberg reporting hundreds affected.
3. **CEO-driven**: Unusual for a Chinese internet giant, Ding personally oversees game division decisions, including hiring finance graduates as direct reports. His shifting priorities reportedly created instability.
4. **Contrast with miHoYo/HoYoverse**: NetEase is public (market pressure), has wider global studio footprint, and has conducted more visible layoffs. Both companies face similar China labor-law constraints on organizing.
