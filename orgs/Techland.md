# Techland — Games Power Map

## Overview

Techland is a Polish video game developer and publisher founded in 1991 by Paweł Marchewka. It rose to prominence with the Call of Juarez series, Dead Island (2011), and the Dying Light franchise. The studio employs 550+ staff primarily at its Wrocław headquarters.

In July 2023, Tencent announced it would acquire a majority stake in Techland — a deal that closed in January 2024 at an estimated valuation of $1.5-1.6 billion (PLN 6.3B). Tencent acquired approximately 67% of shares, making this Poland's largest technology M&A transaction in 2023/2024. Founder Paweł Marchewka retained the remaining ~33% and remains CEO, with the studio reportedly maintaining creative independence and full ownership of its IP.

## Tencent Relationship

The Tencent acquisition is the defining structural event for Techland in the 2022-2026 period:

- **July 2023:** Tencent announced majority stake; Marchewka publicly stated studio retains "full creative freedom" and IP ownership
- **January 2024:** Deal closed; estimated ~67% stake for ~$1.5B (PLN 6.3B) — Poland's largest tech M&A
- **Post-acquisition:** Techland operates as a Tencent subsidiary but maintains independent brand and management. Tencent provides capital for the studio's ambitious AAA fantasy RPG project and Dying Light: The Beast

Techland recorded significant losses in the post-acquisition period: $25M (2023) and $37.4M (2024), partly attributed to cancelled projects and ramp-up costs for next-gen development (C-Engine, UE5). Despite Tencent majority ownership, the company has not pursued cost-cutting layoffs, instead reassigning developers from cancelled projects.

## Labor Profile

### Polish Labor Context

Poland's labor landscape:
- **Union density:** ~12-15% overall (significantly lower than Finland's ~60%)
- **Trade union laws:** Unions can be formed by 10+ employees; multiple unions may operate in one workplace; collective bargaining at enterprise level more common than sector-level
- **Minimum wage:** PLN 4,300/month (2025 est); labor code provides standard protections (40h work week, vacation, sick pay, notice periods)
- **Polish Gamedev Workers Union (PGWU):** Formed October 2023 by CDPR employees under OZZ Inicjatywa Pracownicza; open to all Polish game industry workers; monthly fee 16 PLN

### Techland Unionization

Techland employees joined the **Polish Gamedev Workers Union (Związek Pracowników Branży Gier)** in August 2024 — making Techland the second major Polish studio (after CD Projekt RED, 5th studio counting smaller members) with union representation.

This is a significant development because:
1. **Timing:** Occurred ~7 months after the Tencent acquisition closed — suggesting workers organized partly in response to the change in ownership
2. **Scope:** The PGWU is an inter-workplace union, meaning Techland employees organized within the same union framework as CDPR workers, sharing resources and bargaining power
3. **Management response:** Not publicly reported; but Techland's no-layoff stance despite losses suggests management may be taking a cooperative approach to labor relations

No strikes or public labor disputes have been reported at Techland as of October 2026.

### Layoff Record

Techland has maintained a notable no-layoff record:
- **2019:** 13 layoffs from Polish distribution division closure (pre-acquisition, pre-union)
- **2023-2025:** Zero layoffs despite $62M+ in combined losses and two project cancellations
- This contrasts sharply with most AAA studios during the 2023-2026 industry contraction

### Forced Back-to-Office

In July 2024, Techland mandated a return to office for the majority of staff. CEO Paweł Marchewka framed this as a response to a "transitional period" under Tencent's majority ownership. The move attracted some negative attention in industry press but did not escalate into a public labor dispute.

## Development Pipeline

1. **Dying Light: The Beast** — standalone expansion to Dying Light 2; expected 2025 release
2. **Unannounced AAA open-world fantasy RPG** — described as Techland's "biggest project to date"; uses Unreal Engine 5; building on C-Engine tech
3. **Dying Light 2 ongoing support** — post-launch content updates

The pivot to a major fantasy RPG represents a significant genre departure from the studio's zombie-survival and Western roots, possibly reflecting post-Tencent capital to fund a more ambitious project.
