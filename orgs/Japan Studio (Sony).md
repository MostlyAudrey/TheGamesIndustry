# Japan Studio (Sony)

**Former first-party Sony Interactive Entertainment studio based in Tokyo; historically Sony's home market development arm.** Established 1993 as part of Sony Computer Entertainment Japan (SCEJ). Reorganized in April 2021, effectively marking the end of Sony's internal AAA game development in Japan. Successor studio is [[orgs/Team Asobi]].

## Historical significance

Japan Studio was Sony's internal development presence in its home market. Over its 28-year history (1993-2021), it produced or co-produced dozens of titles spanning:

- **Innovative first-party IP:** Ape Escape, Patapon, LocoRoco, Gravity Rush
- **Artistic collaborations:** Ico, Shadow of the Colossus, The Last Guardian (produced with Fumito Ueda / Team Ico)
- **Co-development support:** Demon's Souls and Bloodborne (co-developed with FromSoftware)
- **Astro Bot:** Astro's Playroom (2020) was the swan song of the original Japan Studio — developed by internal team Team Asobi

## The reorganization (2020-2021)

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| Late 2020 | Sony confirms Japan Studio restructuring amid reports of downsizing |
| Feb 2021 | Key departures: Gravity Rush director Keiichiro Toyama, art director Masami Yamamoto, and producer Junya Okura leave to found Bokeh Game Studio |
| Apr 2021 | Japan Studio formally reorganized; Team Asobi becomes standalone studio |
| Dec 2021 | Sony confirms Japan Studio will focus on IP management and external production, not internal development |

The reorganization was driven by several factors:
- Sony's strategic pivot to Western AAA market (The Last of Us, God of War, Spider-Man)
- Japan Studio's limited commercial output relative to Western first-party studios
- Key talent departures and difficulty retaining creative leads
- Shuhei Yoshida's departure (SIE Worldwide Studios president, 2019)

## Team Asobi: the successor

Main article: [[orgs/Team Asobi]]

Team Asobi, formed in 2012 as an internal division of Japan Studio, was spun out as Sony's sole remaining Japanese first-party studio in April 2021. Under Nicolas Doucet, the studio achieved critical and commercial success with Astro Bot (2024), widely regarded as the spiritual successor to Japan Studio's creative legacy.

## Labor

- **No union:** No union organizing activity documented at Japan Studio
- **No mass layoffs:** Restructuring was managed through voluntary departures and contract non-renewals, not involuntary layoffs
- **Talent exodus:** The reorganization precipitated the departure of several key creative figures who had defined the studio's identity
